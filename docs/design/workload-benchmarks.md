# Structural workload measurements · `rec:design:workload-benchmark-series`

**Convention (Workload inputs)** · `conv:harness:workload-benchmark-inputs` Component curves measure a declared deterministic recipe through public constructors and validators. Correctness is checked before sampling and after the timed series; input construction is excluded, while allocations made by the operation and destruction of its returned owned output remain timed. Consuming marker operations receive a fresh input clone outside each timed iteration. Fixture proof equality measures structural evidence handling, not cryptographic verification or chain finality. A refused, unsupported or incomplete point remains an explicitly typed result and cannot be fitted as successful throughput. Source, configuration, dimensions, limits, setup, raw samples and resource scope accompany every measurement. Each campaign states its resource scopes. The earlier campaign has container cgroup readings only; the all-components campaign additionally records node-wide pressure, load and busy cores from proc, separately from its container cgroup. Waited process accounting gives own CPU. Neither scope attributes work to another login. Every float in the authored carrier uses exponent-free decimal notation, expanding exactly the digits of its shortest round-trip representation. The writer preserves the parsed value, including signed zero; the reader refuses other spellings. Every retained value parses back identically to the campaign raw result, as checked by the round-trip witness. The generated dataset carries those same values in the documents serializer's canonical form; its spelling need not match the authored input. The input rule prevents ambiguous digit runs without rounding evidence or changing the serializer. These curves declare no host workload, production API limit or capacity. Repeated correspondence checks reuse parsed and validated workload rows only when the complete input text is identical. The invocation rereads the file and returns a private projection; any changed byte is a different cache key and is validated afresh. The cache is process-local and bounded, not a persistent approval or a producer digest. Test fixture compaction similarly reuses only identical source text and writes private files. All samples remain committed in the carrier and deterministic dataset, with no rendering arithmetic or uncommitted evidence locator. Cold validation and byte reading remain linear in evidence size; repeated parsing and per-sample family selection are not repeated for unrelated assertion mutations.

**Record (Sampling contract)** · `rec:workload:sampling` Full campaigns use 10 samples, 100 ms warm-up, 1 000 ms measurement targets and 1 000 statistical resamples, with flat iteration scheduling. The profile is gate and the deterministic seed is 0. These are sampling targets. Each current grid also declares an invocation wall bound: expiration retains an incomplete operational point, with no fitted samples, rather than a mathematical FAIL. The bound includes setup, correctness, warm-up and sampling; it does not borrow a gate budget. A serialized pilot precedes a full series. The existing foundation smoke retains its settings; the extra smoke points are declared in the grid carrier. Full campaigns run serially at idle priority on a recorded allowed CPU with a separate build target. Timing is nonverdict under (`rule:devperf:nonverdict`). The dataset names each package-owned construction control. Result association is checked by (`claim:harness:workload-result-binding`).

**Record (Act history curve)** · `rec:workload:act-history` The act grid measures complete-history structural validation with distinct act identities and fixed Actor names over a shared endpoint. Its mixed variant alternates complete binary and paired fixture families. It does not execute the epoch compiler's family formation or establish authentication validity. The recipe and sizes live in the workload dataset; no paper wall bound is inferred for the complete validator.

**Record (Marker history curve)** · `rec:workload:marker-history` The marker grid measures validation and recognition together, with policy and burn binding prepared before timing. The initial marker has an empty accepted set; successors repeat a complete accepted singleton with valid lineage and predecessor links. List and commitment representations are separate curves. The commitment fixture checks the complete opening bytes; no real settlement latency is represented by its synthetic positions.

**Record (Transport byte curve)** · `rec:workload:codec-bytes` The byte grid compares strict canonical byte strings with allowed indefinite transport strings over equal payloads. Empty chunks remain legal and are included. Each transport operation receives explicit input, allocation, item, depth, nesting and work limits from the experiment recipe. The decoder's reservation counter explains requested allocation work; it is not a general allocator or peak-memory measurement.

**Record (Publication history curve)** · `rec:workload:publication-history` The host publication grid measures complete canonical encoding, decoding, and decoding followed by epoch restoration as separate variants. A constructor-backed synthetic retained verifier admits a first boundary with a registration and opinion, followed by two opinions at each successor. The Actor count and acts per boundary stay fixed while the cumulative history grows with boundary count; this is a publication-history curve, not an independent act-set curve. Every point checks disclosure equality, byte fixed point and restored carrier and boundary evidence before and after sampling. The ordinary publication roundtrip case supplies the independent two-boundary control. No digest or decoded flag replaces restoration, and no authentication or deployment capacity claim follows from the fixture. Sizes live in the carrier and span the range needed for a later power-with-offset fit; this implementation records no fitted coefficients or measurements.

**Record (Publication byte curve)** · `rec:workload:publication-bytes` The byte grid varies the realization provenance field under a verifier that checks its exact deterministic bytes before admission. Its other mathematical and authentication causes are fixed. The recipe varies an admitted opaque disclosure, not an invalid signature or a forged checked carrier. Encoded byte length is retained as an exact integer; it is the complete input or output of the codec, not an arithmetic-operation count.

**Record (Storage history curve)** · `rec:workload:storage-history` The storage grid uses the gate's real PostgreSQL fixture with a private database per size and the public typed Rule succession operations. Schema and population precede timing and have separate wall receipts. The prebuilt current-thread runtime uses explicit block_on; timed reads include transaction and driver costs. The service step meters provisioning and shutdown outside the benchmark. The boundary result and its exact expected edition are checked before and after sampling, and history audit must succeed. The ordinary storage contract retains its independent memory/PostgreSQL oracle. This is Rule-history reconstruction; durable admitted-epoch persistence remains held. Successful append calls and returned effective rows are exact public counts; neither is a physical SQL row count.

**Convention (Public workload counts)** · `conv:workload:public-count-scope` Receipts retain exact publicly available counts with their coverage. A sum of published standing replay work covers one retained standing record per boundary, not all invocation work or repeated controller replay. The codec's private arithmetic budget, records' numeric-context ledger, complete restore usage, and physical storage rows read are unavailable through the public interfaces. Those operations retain wall samples without inventing a total work counter. Input counts, partial replay evidence and execution counts remain separately named. No instrumentation or counter accessor is added to the production libraries by these benchmarks.

## Scaling interpretation · `sec:workload:scaling-interpretation`

**Record (Component cost study)** · `rec:workload:paper-cost-comparisons` These are component curves, not capacity claims. The next campaign retains exact consumed Work as its primary scaling quantity where the operation exposes a complete counter, and wall time as secondary evidence. Fits of the form t(n) = k + c·n^p require intervals, goodness of fit and enough distinct successful sizes to test the model; fitting is a later step. Refused and control points are not fit. Actor count, complete acts, nonzero support, search sets and proposal iterations remain distinct dimensions. Arithmetic Work also depends on bit lengths, precision and reuse, so a fitted Work exponent is not automatically an arithmetic-operation exponent.

**Rule (Scaling fixture procedures)** · `rule:workload:scaling-procedures` The solve and replay grid uses a separately declared fixture allowance of 20 000 000 000 Work, rather than the previous 200 000 000 fixture allowance. This reserve is deliberately generous relative to the refused small cases, not a measured deployment capacity. Each point is additionally bounded by 180 seconds including sampling. The integer Actor grid resolves adjacent sizes before its bounded larger extension. The transport oracle has its own allowance of 20 000 000 000 Work, excluded from operation measurements; its operation allowance is 2 000 000 000 Work. The oracle still checks both exact and enclosed results and the independent mean. Rational controller replay uses the same procedure as its constructor and correctness check, at 21 bits and 2 000 000 000 Work. Short inverse caps remain labelled stopping controls, the limited fence remains a resource control, and own-solvency, individual-safety and epoch-policy counterexamples remain gate controls. No assertion or protocol condition is relaxed. The epoch solve_resource_cause witness distinguishes an exhausted request from a genuine declaration error; (`claim:harness:workload-wall-bound-is-operational`) checks deadline propagation without inventing a successful result.

**Record (Counter authority)** · `rec:workload:counter-authority` B4 retains the operation budget delta, excluding budget initialization and the oracle. B6 retains production Work and mandatory replay Work separately, with their sum as the conserved-driver ledger total, excluding separately bounded fence and preflight operations. B7 reports only fresh replay Work as its conserved-driver ledger total, excluding fence and preflight and keeping producer counts as setup evidence. Ridge replay and controller replay retain their fresh execution Work rather than a producer's asserted counter. Fence primitive Work is a labelled partial counter: preparation ledgers are not exposed. Compiler and final-admission APIs discard internal arithmetic ledgers, so no complete Work total is claimed for them. Structural partition, title, record and marker APIs have no Work counter. Strict decoding has no public Work meter; its receipt says unavailable, while bounded transport decoding reports its own identical-read counter. Unavailable is never zero. Failed operations without a complete counter do not inherit a successful point's Work.
