# Lineage concept · `spec:lineage:concept`

## Purpose and standing · `sec:lineage:purpose`

**Convention (Concept boundary)** · `conv:lineage:boundary` This decided and unbuilt concept joins the Actor token, burn and marker in one open covenant lineage under one network key. It assigns no byte recipe, program type, public field or resource value under (`def:vision:concept-boundary`); the stated CBOR envelope and its relay-size bound are properties of the decided form, not an encoding admission. Present codecs, controls and fixtures keep their admitted meanings until their owners admit successors. No execution claim, paper case or external premise is established by this document under (`rule:testing:public-record-conformance`).

**Convention (Vocabulary)** · `conv:lineage:vocabulary` Relations are relative to one finite authenticated chain history under the selected realization rules. Actor and frame gross amount follow the paper; handle and SOURCE/MERGE follow the lineage covenant; intersection, counted intersection and declaration window are deployment terms. A changed chain history is not silently the same relation. Token control identifies neither a person nor possession of content, and a signing delegate does not thereby control the token under (`conv:author:actor`) and (`conv:author:control`).

## Lineage premises · `sec:lineage:premises`

**Definition (Public covenant source)** · `def:lineage:source` The public source of the lineage covenant is https://github.com/proof-commons/tripod at development tag 0.7.226-dev: its fixed-form paper, edition 2.1.0, at papers/lineage-fixed-form/ and its realization record at docs/lineage/realization.md; these documents are dedicated to the public domain under CC0 1.0 Universal in LICENSE-DOCS. This reference supplies only the conceptual premises below, selects no implementation or dependency, and binds no version for a build.

**Assumption (Qualified first output)** · `assum:lineage:qualified-output` A network handle is output zero carrying the authenticated covenant program for a fixed key, asset and deposit triple, with explicit asset and value; a matching program alone is not membership. Supporting place in (`def:lineage:source`), papers/lineage-fixed-form/sections/: 02_model.tex, def:kpcfixed-model:keyed-membership.

**Assumption (Spending condition)** · `assum:lineage:spending` Spending a qualified handle requires the same qualified first output in the spending transaction; the covenant supplies no party authorization and constrains no other output, so ordinary chain validity may admit separate token outputs and one or several destruction outputs beside it. This relies on immutable authenticated fields, distinct programs for distinct triples and enforcement of the whole spending condition without a bypass. Supporting place in (`def:lineage:source`), papers/lineage-fixed-form/sections/: 02_model.tex, def:kpcfixed-model:first-output and assum:kpcfixed-model:realization.

**Assumption (Open extension and joining)** · `assum:lineage:open` Anyone may create a source handle or spend one or several same-triple handles to continue or join their lineage given the authenticated triple, public program recipe and spend data, authenticated history and, for a continuation or join, live input handles, adequate funding and ordinary funding signatures, and transactions satisfying consensus, other input conditions and the realization’s declared relay envelope. Public construction promises no inclusion and bounds neither lost races nor their cost or waiting time; a retry must meet these premises again. Supporting place in (`def:lineage:source`), papers/lineage-fixed-form/sections/: 03_operations.tex, op:kpcfixed-operations:source and op:kpcfixed-operations:merge; 10_limitations.tex, rem:kpcfixed:public-extension.

**Assumption (Handle ancestry)** · `assum:lineage:ancestry` Ancestry follows qualified same-triple handle inputs back to their creating transactions, with immutable event identity and contents; it does not follow arbitrary funding inputs. Continuation is the reflexive transitive successor relation through the qualified first output of a consuming transaction. At a fixed prefix of one valid history, a consumed handle has exactly one successor and each handle has exactly one live descendant; continuation preserves its source-record membership. Supporting place in (`def:lineage:source`), papers/lineage-fixed-form/sections/: 02_model.tex, def:kpcfixed-model:history; 03_operations.tex, op:kpcfixed-operations:merge and def:kpcfixed-operations:continuation; 04_invariants.tex, thm:kpcfixed-invariants:unique-successor and thm:kpcfixed-invariants:set-membership-growth.

**Assumption (Records and joins)** · `assum:lineage:records` A SOURCE introduces a source record; a MERGE introduces no record of its own and unions its input record sets. A published reader fold may read any authenticated history data, including destruction evidence; the covenant neither enforces nor defines those additional meanings. Destruction attributed to a covenant source record must occur in that record’s SOURCE event, and reading other events creates or changes no such record. Supporting place in (`def:lineage:source`), papers/lineage-fixed-form/sections/: 03_operations.tex, op:kpcfixed-operations:source, op:kpcfixed-operations:merge and rem:kpcfixed-operations:reader-folds.

**Assumption (Deposit and surplus)** · `assum:lineage:deposit` Each handle carries the fixed deposit of its triple; joining n handles retains one deposit and leaves the surplus unconstrained by this covenant, with no promised recipient or profit. Supporting place in (`def:lineage:source`), papers/lineage-fixed-form/sections/: 03_operations.tex, prop:kpcfixed-operations:merge-surplus.

**Assumption (Complete reading)** · `assum:lineage:reader` A membership path establishes inclusion, not completeness; missing evidence is incomplete, conflicting identities or cyclic dependencies are invalid, and a nonqualifying output is not a handle. None justifies a smaller supposedly complete record set. Inclusion, retrieval and finality remain evidence premises. Supporting place in (`def:lineage:source`), papers/lineage-fixed-form/sections/: 01_interface.tex, sec:kpcfixed:interface and rem:kpcfixed-interface:dependency-closure.

**Assumption (Realization evidence)** · `assum:lineage:realization` The realization record describes development regtest observations and derived bounds, not signed-block evidence for this deployment; its constructor restrictions do not prove impossibility under the abstract covenant, and source resource constants are not this deployment’s limits. The realization record’s reading section requires attributable destruction in a SOURCE event and explains that MERGE and no-handle events introduce no records. Its scope gives the abstract law to the paper: read with the record-attribution condition and reader-fold permission of (`assum:lineage:records`), that requirement concerns attribution to covenant records, not the separate attribution to Actors defined here; no implementation of this deployment’s fold is inferred. Supporting places in the realization record defined by (`def:lineage:source`): docs/lineage/realization.md, sec:keyed-fixed:scope and sec:keyed-fixed:reading, with its observation and bound sections.

## Objects and rules · `sec:lineage:objects`

**Definition (Lineage)** · `def:lineage:lineage` The network lineage is the family of qualified covenant handles for one authenticated triple q with the network's key, together with their creating transactions and same-triple consumption edges.

**Definition (Lineage transaction)** · `def:lineage:transaction` A lineage transaction is a transaction whose first output is a qualified handle of the network lineage.

**Definition (Descent and reach)** · `def:lineage:reach` For network handles x and y, write x ⪯ y when y is x or is reached from x by finitely many covenant successor steps, and define R(y) as all lineage transactions whose first outputs x satisfy x ⪯ y.

**Definition (Actor token and path)** · `def:lineage:actor` An Actor is a token qualified under the development token rules with its creation-fixed identifier, and its path starts at genesis and follows consecutive supported movements of that same unique unit only up to the first unsupported spend, after which later authority is unavailable even if a later movement is explicit.

**Definition (Declaration)** · `def:lineage:declaration` A declaration on an Actor's path is an effective public key declaration carried by a path transaction under the token's declaration rules, including its authorization of all outputs and its application to each qualified co-moving token.

**Definition (Intersection)** · `def:lineage:intersection` An intersection of an Actor with the lineage is a transaction on that Actor's token path that is also a lineage transaction.

**Definition (Counted intersection)** · `def:lineage:counted` For a marker handle m and the token's intersections i₁,…,iₙ in path order, iⱼ counts exactly when every iₜ with t ≤ j belongs to R(m).

**Definition (Declaration window)** · `def:lineage:window` The window closed by the latest counted intersection iⱼ consists of path transactions strictly after iⱼ₋₁ through iⱼ inclusive, or from genesis through i₁ inclusive when j=1.

**Definition (Epoch key set)** · `def:lineage:keys` An Actor's key set for the epoch generated after a marker is the union of effective declarations in its latest counted window, empty when complete evidence establishes that no intersection counts or that window contains no declaration, and unavailable where the token path is unsupported or evidence incomplete.

**Definition (Burn)** · `def:lineage:burn` A burn is an OP_RETURN destruction output of a lineage transaction whose value is a valid protocol document naming the Actor and whose destroyed amount is in the oracle-selected asset, with each output identified separately from the others in that transaction.

**Rule (Burn reading)** · `rule:lineage:burn-reading` The deployment reads the creating transactions reached through qualified handle ancestry under (`def:lineage:reach`) for burns under (`def:lineage:burn`), including burns in joining transactions, and attributes each destruction to its named Actor rather than to a covenant source record. This reader fold uses the permission of (`assum:lineage:records`), read with (`assum:lineage:realization`); it neither creates covenant records nor substitutes their record-set fold for the deployment’s burn reading.

**Definition (Recognized burn)** · `def:lineage:recognized-burn` A burn in transaction b for Actor a is recognized at marker handle m exactly when b belongs to R(m) and some intersection of a belongs to R(m).

**Definition (Gross map)** · `def:lineage:gross` At every marker including the initial marker, an Actor's chain-derived gross amount is the sum of the destroyed amounts of its distinct recognized burn outputs, zero for an established empty set, and is the amount supplied to the epoch generated after that marker.

**Definition (Marker token)** · `def:lineage:marker-token` The marker token is a distinct unique token, not author-qualified and not an Actor, whose supported movement is authorized only by the host and whose path orders the deployment's marker movements.

**Definition (Marker transaction)** · `def:lineage:marker` A marker transaction is a movement of the marker token carrying the network's qualified first lineage output and the protocol OP_RETURN, which for a following marker names the preceding accepted epoch; that first output is its marker handle and the initial declaration remains a construction hold.

**Definition (Accepted epoch subject)** · `def:lineage:accepted-epoch` The accepted epoch named by a following marker is the root of the epoch’s own static document set, with no reference from that set to the preceding marker, under (`dec:design:epoch-record-order`), with the reference representation owned by identity and publication.

**Definition (Marker error)** · `def:lineage:marker-error` After the initial marker, a marker-token movement is erroneous unless it has a marker handle y and the preceding movement's marker handle x satisfies x ⪯ y; this condition implies descent from every earlier movement by transitivity.

**Rule (Open extension)** · `rule:lineage:extension` Any party may extend or join network handles when it satisfies the covenant, ordinary transaction validity and the selected chain's construction conditions.

**Convention (Window persistence)** · `conv:lineage:window-persistence` The latest counted declaration window and its key set persist across markers until another intersection counts, provided the token path remains supported under (`def:lineage:actor`); after its first unsupported spend authority is unavailable under (`def:lineage:keys`), because later intersections cannot be established on the admitted path and persistence cannot be inferred from their apparent absence. Within that supported path, continuing to act requires no token move merely because another epoch begins.

**Convention (Handle deposit)** · `conv:lineage:deposit` Each network lineage output carries the covenant's fixed deposit, independently of the number of burns in its transaction, and no payout is promised.

**Rule (Authority window)** · `rule:lineage:authority` Every act of an epoch uses that epoch's delegate set for the recorded proposal and approval signatures, and no act carries or applies a retirement-point instruction.

**Rule (Marker halt)** · `rule:lineage:halt` A marker error stops the chain of epochs and requires manual intervention without a predefined recovery procedure.

**Rule (Epoch commitments)** · `rule:lineage:commitments` The designed and unbuilt epoch root commits only its own static document set, not its marker or a preceding epoch, and commits no Actor-to-valid-keys map. The marker names the accepted epoch root and successor standard on the oracle layer. This removes the back-reference from the successor, not from the admitted historical carrier of (`dec:design:epoch-record-order`); ``def:epoch:document-set-commitment`` is closure and ``def:comparator:successor-standard`` is oracle.

**Convention (Check owners)** · `conv:lineage:check-owners` The act-and-epoch document concept is owed by its document owners and must own closure over supplied inputs and oracle assessment over chain history; this concept needs that separation and supplies its lineage and token meanings without minting their check rules. The historical separation remains under (`conv:author:checks`) and (`dec:devnet:author-authority-case`).

**Convention (Protocol payload envelope)** · `conv:lineage:payload` Every protocol OP_RETURN value is a CBOR document with version and namespace of at most 80 bytes, with several such outputs permitted but no operation requiring more than one in a transaction.

**Convention (Asset boundary)** · `conv:lineage:asset` The oracle realization selects the destroyed asset, which is the first network's test coin, without adding that asset selection as a closure input.

**Rule (Incomplete reading)** · `rule:lineage:incomplete` Authenticated handle paths establish membership in R(m), and distinct burns with authenticated amounts, Actor attribution and a reached token intersection establish a gross lower bound whose excess over a committed amount is a discrepancy; exact gross totals, the latest counted intersection, the key set and non-membership require complete applicable evidence, so otherwise incomplete evidence yields inability to establish those results rather than zero.

**Assumption (Nonnegative destruction amounts)** · `assum:lineage:burn-amounts` The chain asset’s destroyed amounts used by (`def:lineage:burn`) are nonnegative quantities with exact addition and comparison; the deployment’s realization must establish this premise without assigning an asset representation here.

**Proposition (Gross growth within one history)** · `prop:lineage:gross-growth` In one fixed valid authenticated history, the chain-derived gross amount for each Actor cannot decrease along a valid marker continuation.

**Proof (Gross growth)** · `pf:lineage:gross-growth` By the non-error condition of (`def:lineage:marker-error`), a valid marker continuation preserves descent; transitivity of (`def:lineage:reach`) retains every earlier reached burn transaction and its witnessing token intersection. Each previously recognized burn therefore remains recognized by (`def:lineage:recognized-burn`). The distinct-output sum in (`def:lineage:gross`) retains each such amount once, and every additional amount is nonnegative by (`assum:lineage:burn-amounts`), proving (`prop:lineage:gross-growth`) for each Actor. A replacement chain history is outside these premises and requires a separate oracle assessment.

**Convention (Relation interpretation)** · `conv:lineage:interpretation` The concept reads “merged, by any path” as reflexive handle succession and reads the listed intersection and burn-recognition conditions as sufficient as well as necessary; these are explicit interpretations of the stated conditions, not additional hidden eligibility tests. “Qualified handle” means covenant program/asset/deposit/position membership; “qualified token” means the distinct issuance and spend rules of the Actor token. A burn's valid document and selected asset are its stated predicates, with no additional undefined qualification. A fold following all funding inputs would include transactions outside R(m) and is not this reading. The reach relation is reflexive, so a burn or token intersection in the marker transaction itself can reach that marker. Token path order is not covenant ancestry order: two successive token intersections may start separate covenant sources. Earlier token transactions need not all be lineage transactions, but their effective declarations still enter the relevant window. Token-path completeness must establish all earlier intersections, not merely the ones discovered under m. A reached later intersection with an unreached predecessor is not counted; it can nonetheless satisfy the weaker burn recognition predicate under (`def:lineage:recognized-burn`), without establishing signing authority.

**Convention (Declared keys)** · `conv:lineage:declared-keys` The public keys in the conceptual set are represented today by declared key hashes matched to disclosed signature keys under (`amend:identity:signing-key-hash`); a hash is not proof of private-key possession. If two markers have the same latest counted intersection, they use the same window and key set. If several intersections become counted together, only the last consecutive window supplies keys; earlier newly counted windows do not enlarge it. A declaration alone beyond that endpoint does not activate a key.

**Rule (Authority evidence)** · `rule:lineage:authority-evidence` To establish a positive authority result, a reader needs the authenticated network triple and chain selection, qualified token genesis, complete supported token path through the relevant pin, complete ordered intersections, authenticated lineage reachability for every earlier intersection through the latest counted one, declaration outputs and their all-outputs authorization, and the act's disclosed signing keys. To prove an intersection is not reached requires complete applicable ancestry or equivalent admitted complete evidence; one successful inclusion proof is insufficient. Whole-path reading can grow from genesis, and third-party neutral extensions increase work without adding a burn. No fixed historical-work bound, compressed proof or client-owned trusted index is assumed.

**Rule (Uniform recognition)** · `rule:lineage:uniform-recognition` Burn recognition at every marker, including the initial marker, uses the same reached-lineage and Actor-intersection predicates, without a requirement that the initial marker reach no burns; recognition is a property of that set rather than the time the marker was made.

**Argument (Window and deposit)** · `arg:lineage:window-deposit` A counted intersection closes one fixed window, so continuing to act needs no token move merely because another epoch begins; a deposit on the handle supports several burns and burns at joins without a per-burn payout rule. One lineage gives the chain reading of burns and key declarations one marker output to start from; the independent host-acceptance and dependency analysis remains held under (`open:lineage:selection`).

**Argument (Marker halt)** · `arg:lineage:halt` A token move off its prior lineage indicates lost discipline or control; stopping under (`rule:lineage:halt`) exposes it rather than silently passing it over. No automatic continuation or advance recovery procedure is designed.

**Convention (Faucet and asset)** · `conv:lineage:faucet` The faucet remains a means for users to obtain test coin, not part of the definition of a burn. Admission of the successor burn rule removes its predecessor's stock bound, pre-opening interval and demand for operator evidence: recognition from the lineage needs none of them. The selected asset remains an oracle matter and is the first network's test coin under (`conv:lineage:asset`); present admissions are unchanged until their replacing cuts.

**Remark (Recognition without authority)** · `rem:lineage:recognition` A reached token intersection can witness a burn without counting for authority when an earlier intersection is not reached; replacing reached with counted would strengthen the burn predicate beyond (`def:lineage:recognized-burn`).

**Remark (Construction boundaries)** · `rem:lineage:construction` The marker token and the lineage handle remain separate outputs of one transaction under (`assum:lineage:spending`); a spend race may require a retry over an enlarged ancestry, with no bound on lost races. The deposit is per handle under (`conv:lineage:deposit`), not per burn, and promises no gatherer payout. An 80-byte value alone proves no complete transaction will relay: funding, output policies, script support and resource limits still apply, and a non-standard policy result is not a consensus-invalidity proof.

**Argument (Origin calculation)** · `arg:lineage:origin-calculation` At network-v0.37.0-draft.2, ``def:comparator:epoch-marker`` says the initial marker has an empty source projection; ``cond:comparator:marker-network-acceptance`` sets A_H(0)=∅; ``eq:comparator:marker-burn-snapshot`` sets B_H^(0)=0 and sums first-common claims from j=1. All three are oracle statements. The deployment's initial recognized source set can instead be nonempty, so its source description at that same marker is not the tagged empty-origin statement. For example, one recognized pre-origin destruction of amount b>0 gives deployment gross b at the initial marker, whereas B_H^(0)=0; those are unequal if a comparison identifies them. There need not be a different value in the first actual epoch calculation. If the release's first positive boundary is supplied exactly the claims recognized by this deployment's initial marker, its B_H^(1)=b equals the first generated epoch's gross b. Closure then performs the same calculation on the same supplied Actor entries under ``def:comparator:frame-inputs`` and ``def:comparator:actor-entry`` (closure). This conditional equality does not establish that the boundaries are the same objects or that the oracle recognizes them under the same rules. A mere renumbering proves neither: publication and development-profile design must give the epoch/boundary association and common-claim correspondence, including pre-origin burns, under M5 and the snapshot definition. Until then, the design states uniform deployment recognition and deterministic calculation over supplied amounts; no conformance claim is made at these labels, including realization of the empty initial source projection or unconditional equality of oracle outputs. This is the design boundary under (`rule:testing:public-record-conformance`), not a change to the paper or an exception suppressing recognized initial burns.

**Definition (Commitment subjects)** · `def:lineage:commitment-subjects` The lineage subject commits the authenticated reached handle history and its attributed destructions, while the host-acceptance subject selects an epoch record by marker-token movement and the record's gross Actor map.

**Remark (Distinct commitment subjects)** · `rem:lineage:commitment-1` These are semantic subjects, not admitted encodings or two new document fields. Anyone may create or extend the first subject without host approval. The second needs the host's token authority and an exact preceding record under (`dec:design:epoch-record-order`). Neither a burn's existence nor a valid handle spend alone selects that record. Conversely a committed amount does not establish a destruction or a complete lineage. The marker selecting a record is the following marker, not a self-commitment by the record's opening marker; the record's amounts are compared against the lineage view of the marker that record names. Newly gathered burns at the selecting marker are not silently added to the preceding record's amounts.

**Definition (Common projection)** · `def:lineage:common-projection` The joint oracle projection contains only full source claims supported both by the lineage subject and by the host-acceptance subject, where agreement includes destruction identity, amount and Actor attribution.

**Remark (Common claims)** · `rem:lineage:commitment-3` A claim on one side only is inert in this projection; it does not erase a chain burn or alter a completed closure calculation. This joint projection is distinct from chain recognition under (`def:lineage:recognized-burn`): every recognized burn remains in the chain-derived gross map, which the oracle compares with the committed map. An unmatched claim cannot be added to the common projection by repairing either operand. A discrepancy between the complete chain-derived map and the committed map can therefore be reported without silently replacing the epoch's supplied calculation inputs.

**Remark (Aggregate information)** · `rem:lineage:commitment-4` The release's ``cond:comparator:marker-network-acceptance`` requires full claims containing source-event identity, magnitude and attribution, and ``cond:comparator:marker-single-count`` counts source identities once; both are oracle material. A map from Actor to gross amount loses source identity: two distinct burns of equal amount for one Actor can give the same map. Matching aggregate totals alone therefore does not prove that the full claim sets intersect as asserted. The design owners must specify how host acceptance determines the same source claims, from the committed record's references and applicable public rules, without treating the lineage's answer as an independently committed selection merely by naming it twice. No extra list, digest or proof object is invented here, and no information-equivalence theorem is assumed.

**Remark (Commitment independence)** · `rem:lineage:independence` Distinct public-extension and host-selection authorities establish distinct semantic subjects, not independent dependency cones: transaction identity binds all outputs, marker transactions name preceding records and those records name opening markers. Separate commitments may share full claims. Exact subjects, dependencies and claim correspondence remain with publication and identity under (`open:lineage:selection`); no departure is declared merely because one lineage is read and no realization is claimed before those obligations are shown.

## Consequences · `sec:lineage:consequences`

**Proposition (Single contribution)** · `prop:lineage:single-contribution` Each destruction output in the defined gross map contributes at most once to one Actor.

**Proof (Single contribution)** · `pf:lineage:single-contribution` Immutable transaction identity and output position distinguish destructions; the gross sum ranges over distinct outputs and their fixed attribution. This follows from the definitions, not a new invariant; the oracle reader still owes complete evidence and distinct-output evaluation.

**Proposition (Authority prefix)** · `prop:lineage:authority-prefix` The intersections counted at a marker form an initial segment of each Actor token's ordered intersection path.

**Proof (Authority prefix)** · `pf:lineage:authority-prefix` The counted-intersection definition requires every earlier intersection to reach the same marker handle. This follows from the definition, not a new invariant; the oracle reader still owes complete evidence and counted-prefix evaluation.

## Cases · `sec:lineage:cases`

**Example (First intersection)** · `ex:lineage:first-intersection` Genesis-to-intersection declarations become eligible when it reaches the marker. Decided; genesis construction remains open.

**Example (Declare, then intersect)** · `ex:lineage:declare-then-intersect` Declarations before or on that intersection enter its window; declaration alone does not activate a key. Decided.

**Example (Change keys)** · `ex:lineage:change-keys` A new counted intersection closes a new window; keys absent from it cease to authorize the following epoch. Decided; the latest consecutive window supplies the set, not a union of windows.

**Example (Window with no declaration)** · `ex:lineage:window-with-no-declaration` No key authorizes an act until a new counted intersection with declarations. Decided.

**Example (Declarations after latest counted intersection)** · `ex:lineage:declarations-after-latest-counted-intersection` No effect on this epoch's authority. Decided.

**Example (Later intersection merged, earlier missing)** · `ex:lineage:later-intersection-merged-earlier-missing` Later does not count; latest fully reached initial segment determines keys. Decided.

**Example (Burn before any counted intersection)** · `ex:lineage:burn-before-any-counted-intersection` It can wait; when a reached intersection exists, the burn is recognized if its own lineage also reaches the marker. Decided predicates; reached does not mean counted.

**Example (Third party burns for Actor)** · `ex:lineage:third-party-burns-for-actor` Attribution follows the output identifier, not the funder's identity; no signature delegation follows from paying. Decided, with token qualification required.

**Example (Several burns in one transaction)** · `ex:lineage:several-burns-in-one-transaction` Each output is a separate destruction; none duplicates another by sharing a transaction. Decided; ordinary validity and representation admission remain.

**Example (Burn in marker transaction)** · `ex:lineage:burn-in-marker-transaction` Reflexive reach includes it, provided the Actor-intersection predicate holds. Reflexive interpretation applies at the first marker too; transaction construction remains design work.

**Example (Old key valid last epoch, absent now)** · `ex:lineage:old-key-valid-last-epoch-absent-now` Oracle does not authorize it now; a cryptographically valid signature alone is not local admission. Decided.

**Example (Newly declared key not counted)** · `ex:lineage:newly-declared-key-not-counted` Same refusal of authority, without deleting the declaration or changing older acts. Decided.

**Example (No new counted intersection at next marker)** · `ex:lineage:no-new-counted-intersection-at-next-marker` Same latest consecutive window, hence the same key set. Decided persistence.

**Example (Third parties grow ancestry)** · `ex:lineage:third-parties-grow-ancestry` Reading and retry costs grow; no partial walk may be labelled complete. Decided permission plus evidence consequence.

**Example (Token lost)** · `ex:lineage:token-lost` The latest counted key set persists without a new token move; loss of token control alone does not revoke those keys. Decided persistence; no recovery promise and unsupported spends still end the supported path.

**Example (Token under another's control)** · `ex:lineage:token-under-another-s-control` That control can authorize later declarations/intersections, not reassign prior burn or alter earlier recorded acts. Concept consequence of permanent identity and windows.

**Example (Chain reorganization)** · `ex:lineage:chain-reorganization` Re-evaluate applicable oracle evidence; old closure results remain calculations over old inputs. Paper oracle rule; deployment finality response remains separately owned.

**Example (First marker)** · `ex:lineage:first-marker` No predecessor movement to compare; its reached burns are recognized by the same predicates as at every marker and supply the following epoch's gross amounts. Decided recognition; issuance construction and release-boundary correspondence remain design work.

**Example (Burn existing before the first marker)** · `ex:lineage:burn-existing-before-the-first-marker` Recognized there when its burn and Actor-intersection lineage outputs are reached; included in the epoch after that marker, without a temporal exclusion. Decided uniform recognition.

**Example (Several burns or a burn at a merge)** · `ex:lineage:several-burns-or-a-burn-at-a-merge` One deposit belongs to the resulting lineage output, not each burn; no payout is owed. Decided deposit convention.

**Example (Off-lineage marker movement)** · `ex:lineage:off-lineage-marker-movement` Stop epoch chain, publish no automatic continuation; manual intervention not specified. Decided halt; intervention not said.

**Example (Source or merge with no burn)** · `ex:lineage:source-or-merge-with-no-burn` Zero burn contribution, while its ancestry and token intersections still matter. Concept consequence.

**Example (Missing token path or missing merge input)** · `ex:lineage:missing-token-path-or-missing-merge-input` Valid partial paths retain their membership evidence and recognized burns supply a lower bound; an excess over the committed amount is a discrepancy, while exact totals and authority remain unavailable. Paper evidence distinction and proposed incomplete rule.

## Owned holds · `sec:lineage:holds`

**Open Question (Origin correspondence)** · `open:lineage:origin` Owner: publication and development-profile designers. Uniform recognition under (`rule:lineage:uniform-recognition`) permits a nonempty initial reached set, whereas network-v0.37.0-draft.2 ``def:comparator:epoch-marker`` requires empty initial sources, M2 empty initial acceptance and the snapshot equation zero initial gross; the deployment's first subsequent epoch includes those burns, so publication and oracle design must state the boundary correspondence and compare actual inputs rather than claim equality at boundary zero or silently discard those burns.

**Open Question (Commitment realization)** · `open:lineage:selection` Owner: publication and identity designers. The lineage and host acceptance are distinct subjects under (`def:lineage:commitment-subjects`), and separate commitments may share claims; publication and identity owe their exact committed objects, dependency-cone analysis and full-claim projection under network-v0.37.0-draft.2 ``post:comparator:marker-bound-finalization``, because an aggregate amount map does not identify destructions and the marker transaction's record reference creates dependencies that distinct control alone does not remove; no departure or realized independent-commitment case is claimed. This design must bind the accepted static root and successor standard without an epoch back-reference, and must supply the separately selected input correspondence and static verdict; none is established by the existing local whole-set refusals.

**Open Question (Envelope and declaration continuity)** · `open:lineage:envelope` Owner: identity and development-profile designers. Preserving the declaration rules literally would preserve the exact non-CBOR PNAT2/PNAK1 scripts, whereas the new form requires every protocol OP_RETURN value to be versioned and namespaced CBOR; the concept preserves declaration semantics and holds the replacement encoding with identity and development, including the construction of qualification and first declarations without requiring two protocol outputs for one operation.

**Open Question (Reader limits)** · `open:lineage:limits` Owner: reader and resource-admission owners. (`req:design:limits`) requires bounded execution while third parties can grow ancestry and token history without a fixed bound; a local limit can report inability but cannot justify a truncated complete epoch, and no liveness guarantee or compressed proof is assumed.

**Remark (Marker intervention)** · `rem:lineage:intervention` Marker error requires manual intervention but supplies no authorized actor, reset, new token, re-anchor or continuity rule; the old policy's reviewed re-anchor is not automatically that intervention, and no recovery procedure is designed in advance under (`rule:lineage:halt`).

**Open Question (Complete realization)** · `open:lineage:realization` Owner: development-profile, wallet and host designers. Marker-token genesis and custody, authenticated network triple, full token-path acquisition, supported transaction constructor, settlement view, signed-block evidence and invalid-payload interpretation remain realization work; the covenant's narrow constructor and regtest observations discharge none of this deployment's combined operation.

**Open Question (Identifier qualification)** · `open:lineage:identifier` Owner: identity designers. The Actor definition names the token identifier directly while the existing Actor spelling is run-qualified; within a selected network the token identifier can identify the Actor, but global cross-run spelling and the 80-byte envelope remain identity admissions, not an implicit removal of run qualification.

**Open Question (Marker commitment placement)** · `open:lineage:marker-placement` Owner: publication and identity designers. The successor epoch root contains neither marker nor predecessor; lineage design owes the marker’s root and successor-standard acceptance encoding and dependency analysis. The admitted historical association of (`dec:design:epoch-record-order`) retains its carrier meaning until replacement; the exact successor partition and reference recipe remain unassigned.

**Open Question (Document concept)** · `open:lineage:documents` Owner: act-and-epoch document concept designers. The successor documents supply acts, one approval witness each, frame gross amounts and sufficient static inputs, with no marker, parent or valid-key map; the adapted act-and-epoch concept is owed. Its static and declared-history checks have distinct inputs and verdicts, and this concept assigns neither their members’ encodings nor a new payload partition or reference recipe.
