# Documents of acts and epochs · `spec:actepoch:concept`

**Table (Documents, layers and verdicts)** · `tab:actepoch:overview` The static verdict says “The epoch as given is internally consistent” and includes completed checks and unavailable private checks; the history verdict says “The declared run is accounting-consistent across its checked seams” and reports whether the empty initial boundary was checked. The first reads one static set under its fixed public specification; the second compares computed states of already validated epochs in a supplied order. Neither establishes chain truth or signing authority. The separate oracle assesses those subjects under (`spec:lineage:concept`). Common documents have fixed counts, order and 32-byte content members; subject documents use their plain shapes. The Actor document is outside the epoch; its opaque hash alone names the Actor inside it. All successor subjects are designed and unbuilt.

| Document / layer | Members and role | Refers to / checks |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Actor — subject, outside the set | Asset identifier and 32-byte blinder, namespace and version | Hash names the Actor; oracle opens it, checks prefix and one accepted document per token at a time |
| Act — common | Author name, sequence, type, typed part, parents, content, dependencies | Seven 32-byte members; no retirement or host member |
| Witnessed act — common | Act hash, signing key, signature half one, half two | Four 32-byte members; complete-act approval under the recorded key |
| Typed and public parts | Type coordinates, incidence, optional license, settlement and public parents | Nineteen act types; required public documents inside the set |
| Removable parts | Act identity, client randomness, content or dependencies | Commitments stay; private openings are not required public inputs |
| Acts | Ordered pairs of act hash and witnessed-act hash | Pair equality; containment supplies completion, position is place |
| Epoch — common | Acts, frame | Two references; no counter, predecessor, marker, signature or seal |
| Frame — subject | Actor-document hash to cumulative gross amount | Keys and amounts 32 bytes; gross is unsigned big-endian minimal-unit count; no Actor document, unit member or authority map |
| Entering state and selected evidence | Cumulative facts for touched subjects, including undated branch, settlement and challenge facts, and irreducible inputs | Derive outgoing values; no stored epoch number |
| Epoch root — common | Epoch, entering state, selected inputs/evidence | Three references; complete static set, no computed-copy branch |
| Static and history closure | Internal validity / declared-run accounting consistency | History receives validated epochs and their computed states; no parent link |
| Oracle and optional displays | Accepted roots, authority and chain gross / computed copies | Oracle marker points inward; courtesy copies outside root, consumer/name mapping held |

## Purpose · `sec:actepoch:purpose`

**Formulation (Purpose)** · `formul:actepoch:purpose` A holder can check one epoch without fetching its past or relying on a host service. Static closure establishes internal validity of its supplied set; a separate declared run permits accounting comparisons across seams. Oracle recognition establishes neither verdict by itself. Authored inputs, cumulative entering facts, independent observations and computed outputs have distinct subjects. This extends (`model:design:epoch-query-publication`) within (`rec:xchg:concept-record`).

**Convention (Historical boundary)** · `conv:actepoch:history` This decided and unbuilt concept concerns successor documents. Historical publication bytes, assigned theories, recipes and controls retain their meanings under (`inv:api:document-bytes`) and (`rule:identity:recipe-permanence`). Removing a member from a successor does not remove it from an admitted object. Paper references are to network-v0.37.1. References in the lineage area name statements of the separate lineage concept, (`spec:lineage:concept`), rather than declarations minted here; their direction is used without claiming its successor realization implemented. Current local policies and conditional claims remain under (`dec:devnet:author-authority-case`), (`amend:devnet:marker-settlement`) and (`rule:testing:public-record-conformance`).

## Principles · `sec:actepoch:principles`

**Invariant (Static closure)** · `inv:actepoch:static-closure` A static check reads only the epoch root’s complete committed input set under the identified fixed public specification; it reads no earlier epoch, external history or current host service. Its verdict is internal consistency of the supplied data, with completed checks and unavailable private checks explicitly listed. Missing required public input prevents confirmation. Every earlier-state operand needed by this calculation is supplied as cumulative entering state. The argument is that a verdict restricted to the data read cannot establish a historical fact, while supplied state permits reproducible calculation after earlier private material is removed. This eliminates traversal, not possible state growth. History accounting and oracle assessment have separate inputs and verdicts under (`rule:actepoch:parent-connection`) and (`rule:actepoch:oracle-checking`).

**Invariant (Independent common members)** · `inv:actepoch:independent-members` A common document has no semantic member that is null, constant or determined by another member of that document. The common documents are the act, witnessed act, epoch and epoch root under (`def:actepoch:document-grade`). Every reader of the corresponding act or epoch handles these roots, so a single shape makes their comparison and interpretation uniform without duplicate descriptions that can disagree. Sub-documents instead state their subject directly: an optional member, integer, ratio or list is permitted where forcing uniformity would add a document or type without adding a fact. Envelope discriminants and binding representation are not semantic members under (`def:actepoch:subject-membership`); their interchange rules remain distinct.

**Definition (Common and subject documents)** · `def:actepoch:document-grade` Common documents are the shared roots through which every act or epoch is identified and related: act, witnessed act, epoch and epoch root. A later document is common when interpreting every instance of the corresponding act or epoch requires its root regardless of the subject a reader selects; a document reached to inspect one particular subject is a sub-document. This is a role test, not a count of clients or requests: being required somewhere in complete replay does not make an evidence part common. Common roots keep (`inv:actepoch:independent-members`) and (`conv:actepoch:fixed-width`) strictly, because their shape is paid for everywhere. Typed acts, public and removable parts, result groups and evidence parts use their subject's plain shape, avoiding extra types or references that add no fact.

**Definition (Subject membership)** · `def:actepoch:subject-membership` A semantic member is a fact whose change changes the subject the document describes. Envelope framing and private random values bind the interpretation or opening without each being a separate semantic member. This distinction separates what a document says from all the bytes a witness must cover.

**Rule (Membership and witness coverage)** · `rule:actepoch:subject-membership` Place a semantic member in a document exactly when changing it changes that document's subject. Apply the independent-member and document-grade rules to its form. A witness covers the exact document bytes, including its envelope and any private random value: omitting binding representation would permit the same apparent statement to be read or opened differently.

**Convention (Common content shape)** · `conv:actepoch:fixed-width` Every content member of a common document is 32 bytes, with fixed count and order: act seven, witnessed act four, epoch two and epoch root three. These widths are the decided form, so readers check one shape before opening its references; no common member is optional or variable. Keys, signature halves and sequences do not become hashes merely by having that width. Subject documents use their plain shapes. The envelope follows interchange, including (`inv:interchange:patch-identity`); no fixed total encoded length is claimed.

**Invariant (Independent parts)** · `inv:actepoch:part-separation` A common document refers by hash to its lists, maps and removable parts as separate documents. Each removable part and each public part with its own retrieval or retention subject has its own document. Public permanent calculation parts carry no random value; removable parts carry independent private random values generated by their author’s client, and their references survive removal. This distinguishes a removed opening from an empty one. The Actor naming document outside the static set has separate prefix-binding randomness under (`def:actepoch:actor-document`), not concealment of a calculation part. A subject document may contain the list or map directly expressing its subject under (`def:actepoch:document-grade`).

**Invariant (Type placement)** · `inv:actepoch:type-placement` A member whose presence or shape depends on act type belongs to the typed document; the common document names the type and refers to that document. The reason is that readers must see the structural distinction in the type rather than guess it from validation success. Interpretation and relational validity still need their owning rules; a hash reference alone establishes neither endpoint validity nor authority.

**Invariant (No extension member)** · `inv:actepoch:no-extension` A document has no opaque extension member. New meaning is given a further admitted version, under (`inv:interchange:minor-inclusion`) and (`law:interchange:major-boundary`). The reason is that an unclassified byte bag supplies no shared interpretation for a witnessed assertion. Removable authored content is a defined subject and is not such an extension. The designed successor version admits no extension bytes: an act bearing them is outside that version and must be refused as unadmitted, with no conformance claimed for that case under (`rule:testing:public-record-conformance`).

**Invariant (Witness separation)** · `inv:actepoch:witness-separation` A signature is held in a witness document beside its subject, never inside the document it signs. The reason is that authored bytes remain comparable independently of possession of a witness or the scheme used to witness them. Replacing or withholding a copy of a witness does not replace the historical witness selected by an accepted epoch. Separation of the approval document does not omit the approval projection from the complete recorded association.

**Rule (Core membership and retained copies)** · `rule:actepoch:computed-publication` The core commits authored and supplied calculation inputs, L cumulative entering state needed to avoid earlier-epoch reads, and V irreducible selections or observations required for the calculation. Closing values are computed outputs, not independent operands or required published copies. A subsequent epoch can supply those values as its entering state; history closure compares the semantic values. A published copy of a computed result is kept where a consumer reads it, as an optional public-interface answer under a vendor-namespaced name and as a courtesy. It may refer inward but is no member of a common document or the root, carries no authority in acceptance and has no conformance claimed under (`rule:testing:public-record-conformance`). A copy adds convenience rather than evidence because its value follows from the root; a vendor-namespaced answer can be served, changed or withdrawn without changing a document’s shape or a repository claim. The floor of network-v0.37.1 is this static set with the public calculation source, under ``def:deployment:published-epoch-replay-floor`` (closure); there is no independent F-only branch. realizes{``def:deployment:published-epoch-replay-floor``; This designs complete public static input carriage and separates optional computed copies; it asserts no implemented successor or oracle truth.}

**Invariant (Host-free authorship)** · `inv:actepoch:host-free-authorship` The act and its Actor witness contain nothing contributed by a host. The author forms the commitments and approves the complete act; the epoch has neither signature nor seal. Host service evidence has a separate subject, so it can vary without changing the authored statement or static root. Historical local constructions retain their admissions under (`conv:actepoch:history`); successor approval admission remains (`open:actepoch:host-association`).

## Act documents · `sec:actepoch:act`

**Definition (Author and type)** · `def:actepoch:author-type` An author name is the hash of the Actor document under (`dec:identity:actor-document-name`). Acts and frame keys use that same opaque 32-byte name; the asset identifier itself is learned only by opening the separate Actor document on the oracle side. A type-specification document states the admitted type’s meaning and members independently of any act instance and is present in the static set. Its name belongs to a closed admitted roster: fetching a specification cannot admit it. Type specifications and assigned interchange theories have distinct roles.

**Definition (Actor naming document)** · `def:actepoch:actor-document` An Actor document binds a network’s name for an Actor to its single-unit asset. Its members are the chain-derived 32-byte asset identifier and a 32-byte random blinder, with the document’s full namespace and version, under (`dec:identity:actor-document-name`). It is a subject document, not a common root: it is opened for the naming subject and is never a required static input. It has no chain payload byte budget and never goes on the chain. The Actor varies the blinder until the document hash has the three-byte prefix assigned to its namespace and version; the blinder binds that representation without becoming another semantic fact about the asset. Prefix validity is checked when the document is opened, not by the epoch’s static checker. The Actor supplies the document to its host; it is not private and need not be published. Within one network the oracle accepts one Actor document for a token at any time and uses that document to relate a burn's raw asset identifier to a frame's Actor name. It refuses a second document opening to the same asset unless the document arrives through a succession flow the oracle admits; such a flow replaces the accepted document and retains the earlier name beside the new under (`amend:identity:actor-document-succession`). No such flow is designed or admitted, so every second document is refused until (`open:identity:actor-document-succession`) is discharged. A later version of what defines an Actor changes its document and prefix, so its names can be distinguished and sorted without being opened. No Actor document, asset-to-name table or key-authority map belongs to the static set. The representation and candidate recipe are designed in (`amend:identity:actor-document-design`); the test namespace is assigned in (`schema:identity:actor-document`), production allocation remains (`open:identity:actor-document-namespaces`), and registry publication, recipe and validity-operation admission remain (`open:identity:actor-document-admission`) under (`req:identity:admission-record`). The decided members and role do not admit that representation.

**Convention (Actor-name prefix assignments)** · `conv:actepoch:actor-prefix` The Actor-document design records the table from three-byte prefix to namespace and version. Its first test assignment is 00 00 00 for org.peer-network.test.actor at major 1 and minor 0; 00 00 01 is reserved for the first real-network version without namespace text or a production theory. This table is absent from epoch documents. Static checking treats every Actor name as opaque, without testing its prefix; opening and uniqueness checks belong to the oracle and the Actor-document admission. The test namespace and concrete theory design stand in (`schema:identity:actor-document`); production allocation remains (`open:identity:actor-document-namespaces`), and neither prefix is an implemented identity admission. The representation design records these as namespace/theory-coordinate rows shared across patch values under (`tab:identity:actor-prefixes`); patch does not define another theory, while its bytes remain part of the document hash.

**Definition (Common act)** · `def:actepoch:act` The common act identifies what the Actor authored and points to its parts. Its seven 32-byte members, in order, are author name, sequence, act type, typed-document reference, parents-document reference, content-document reference and dependency-document reference. The author name is (`def:actepoch:actor-document`)’s hash, not a chain asset or key. Sequence identifies an author-local slot; the act identifier is the author/sequence/type triple, while its document hash names one exact realization. No retirement point, host or external run locator is carried. The oracle assesses the recorded key under (`rule:lineage:authority`). The envelope is not one of the seven. These successor meanings do not reinterpret admitted signed carriers under (`open:actepoch:authority-boundary`). Its representation is designed by (`schema:identity:common-act`), with exact-byte binding admitted as design for pure static operations under (`amend:identity:common-static-admission`) and (`def:identity:common-document-recipes`); the test namespace is assigned by (`schema:identity:common-document-parameters`), while registry evidence and coordinated application enablement remain (`open:identity:common-document-admission`). The act document is the signing subject; the complete recorded act of network-v0.37.1 ``def:graph:act-projections`` (closure) is its resolved association with the paired witnessed act, including all eight semantic projections. The field named sequence supplies the act nonce and carries no ordering meaning. This deployment refuses a repeated author/sequence slot, a local restriction stronger than the paper’s author/nonce/family uniqueness; it is not claimed as the paper’s complete acceptance domain. No stored member, hash recipe or approval changes.

**Proposition (Publish identity)** · `prop:actepoch:publish-refinement` Publish remains one act type because its minting incidence already distinguishes the license-bearing case. Splitting it would refine the type coordinate of the act identifier under (`amend:identity:act-stops`) for a fact already given by incidence under (`alg:design:formation`). An optional member in the typed document expresses that fact under (`inv:actepoch:independent-members`) without imposing another identifier distinction on every reader. The license follows (`rule:actepoch:publish-license`); the shared author/sequence slot gains no second allocation space.

**Rule (Publish license presence)** · `rule:actepoch:publish-license` Publish's typed document holds an optional license member, present exactly when the act mints its Content and absent otherwise. Incidence determines that presence under (`alg:design:formation`); the license is the exact attribution/provenance degree pair retained across payload removal. This states the minting fact once without splitting Publish's identifier type or using a null placeholder.

**Definition (Typed acts)** · `def:actepoch:typed-acts` The type's document holds its varying semantic coordinates, legs reference and applicable license or settlement members. Ratios retain exact authored numerator and denominator under (`amend:identity:formation-three`); no unit-one or vintage-zero constants are carried as independent choices. Registration, Leave, Withdraw, Rescind and De-invite have no semantic coordinate. Publish and Owner have attachment; Opinion polarity/reaction; Affinity association/attraction; Join Request formality/urgency; Accept and Ratify comfort/equity; Participant continuity/interactivity/responsibility; Invitation formality/relevance/urgency; Tag confidence/relevance; Review and Reference effort/enthusiasm; Bid generosity/urgency; Send importance. Accept carries settles and Ratify carries settles and accepts as exact references with the successor’s cited-state structural-completion meaning; exact typed-subject admission remains owed. No layout member is stored when type determines it. The nineteen types retain the nineteen families without creating extra actions or independently debit-able legs; Publish's license follows (`rule:actepoch:publish-license`). realizes{``amend:identity:formation-three``; This uses exact authored coordinate pairs, not private entropy, custody or production selection.}

**Definition (Public parts)** · `def:actepoch:public-parts` A legs document states the incidence of the act's one binary leg or two ordered A/T projections; the shared middle and role relation are not independently contradictory fields. A parents document states the complete distinct asserted-parent list; settlement references contribute to the derived causal-parent relation without another stored union. A protocol document states a nonconstant public-reference list when an admitted type needs it. Current supported types require the protocol list empty, so they contain no redundant constant-empty protocol reference. Public list documents may be shared by exact content where no act-specific field is needed; the empty parents list is a real empty document, never a missing reference. These documents remain available to ordinary formation and retained structural replay. Asserted references and the counted parent relation are distinct: network-v0.37.1 ``def:graph:counted-parent-relation`` (closure) counts only a parent completed before its child, in an earlier epoch or an earlier current causal layer; a forward citation gains no counted edge from later completion.

**Definition (Removable parts)** · `def:actepoch:removable-parts` A content document holds the act identifier, its client's private random value and the authored content. A dependency document holds that identifier, an independent client random value and the complete dependency list. Empty content or a complete empty list remains an opening with its own binding representation, not absence. The two leaves cannot be shared between different act identifiers. Removal leaves the common document, its references and witness unchanged; a tombstone is holder state outside this document set. The membership criterion concerns the authored value; private randomness supplies concealment framing under (`def:actepoch:subject-membership`). No host generates or contributes these values.

**Definition (Witnessed act)** · `def:actepoch:witnessed-act` A witnessed act identifies the complete act and the key under which its approval verifies. Its four 32-byte members are act-document hash, public verification key and signature’s first and second halves, in that order; the act hash is the signed message. The key records who signed, not authorization for an Actor. Static checking verifies the signature under this key; oracle assessment tests it against the epoch’s delegate set under (`rule:lineage:authority`). A negative authority result does not rewrite the act or its calculation. There is no host, receipt, seal, retirement, position or time member. The complete-act message and recorded-key verification are admitted as pure-operation design by (`amend:identity:common-static-admission`); signing and complete subject binding remain held, and no successor signed-preparation or historical-role witness is required. Its representation is designed by (`schema:identity:common-witnessed-act`), with exact-byte binding admitted as design for pure static operations under (`amend:identity:common-static-admission`) and (`def:identity:common-document-recipes`); the test namespace is assigned by (`schema:identity:common-document-parameters`), while registry evidence and coordinated application enablement remain (`open:identity:common-document-admission`).

**Rule (Host realization and conformance)** · `rule:actepoch:host-conformance` At network-v0.37.1, ``def:graph:author-commitment-preparation``, ``def:graph:verified-act`` and ``def:graph:approval-witness`` (closure) describe author-formed commitments and one complete-act approval. The static floor, ``def:deployment:published-epoch-replay-floor`` (closure), requires no host entropy, seal or signed preparation. Exact successor approval and opening associations remain (`open:actepoch:host-association`). Historical proposal/header/seal/approval constructions retain their own meanings and local duties. Under (`rule:testing:public-record-conformance`), signature validity establishes neither custody, entropy nor key-to-Actor authority; the courtesy ``def:graph:courtesy-handshake`` supplies no closure conformance claim. realizes{``def:deployment:published-epoch-replay-floor``; This states the successor public-input duty without importing historical host constructions; exact successor approval admission remains held.}

## Epoch documents · `sec:actepoch:epoch`

**Definition (Public source and selected evidence)** · `def:actepoch:source-evidence` Fixed public specification semantics interpret the supplied calculation inputs. They are an explicit checker premise, not a mutable external locator in the epoch. Every selected version, constant, numerical point or observation not fixed by that specification is supplied inside the static set and bound to its subject. Derived consequences are not independent evidence. Oracle declarations, histories and warrantors are not calculation operands under network-v0.37.1 ``def:comparator:frame-inputs`` and ``post:deployment:replay-floor-scope`` (closure). Exact interpretation and operand binding remain (`open:actepoch:source-selection`).

**Definition (Epoch)** · `def:actepoch:epoch` An epoch document names its accepted acts and supplied frame. Its two 32-byte members are acts-document reference and frame-document reference, in that order. It has no counter, ordinal, predecessor, marker, signature or seal. Position is place in the acts list and count is its length. The root of its own set names no external boundary witness under network-v0.37.1 ``def:epoch:document-set-commitment`` (closure). Markers name accepted roots from outside; the earlier carrier’s marker association remains that carrier’s admitted meaning under (`dec:design:epoch-record-order`). Act sequence slots are independent of epoch numbering. Its representation is designed by (`schema:identity:common-epoch`), with exact-byte binding admitted as design for pure static operations under (`amend:identity:common-static-admission`) and (`def:identity:common-document-recipes`); the test namespace is assigned by (`schema:identity:common-document-parameters`), while registry evidence and coordinated application enablement remain (`open:identity:common-document-admission`).

**Definition (Acts)** · `def:actepoch:acts` The acts document is the complete ordered list of accepted acts and witnesses in the host’s selected order. Each entry pairs the 32-byte act hash first with the 32-byte witnessed-act hash second; the latter’s act member must equal the first. One act occurs once and projections remain one action. Containment identifies completion and co-epochal membership; position is place, without a stored completion ordinal. Earlier placement facts needed by static validation are supplied in entering state. The empty list is a real document; chunking must preserve one finite complete authoritative order. Removing a private opening changes neither pair member. network-v0.37.1 ``def:graph:act-completion-epoch`` and ``def:graph:authoritative-act-order`` are closure statements; ordinal and boundary comparisons have the scoped treatment of (`rule:actepoch:parent-connection`). Its representation is designed by (`schema:identity:common-acts`), with exact-byte binding admitted as design for pure static operations under (`amend:identity:common-static-admission`) and (`def:identity:common-document-recipes`); the test namespace is assigned by (`schema:identity:common-document-parameters`), while registry evidence and coordinated application enablement remain (`open:identity:common-document-admission`). Required public settlement and anchor support resolves inside this static set; earlier counted-reference dispositions are entering facts under (`rule:actepoch:title-prior-facts`), not earlier-epoch locators.

**Definition (Frame)** · `def:actepoch:frame` The frame maps Actor names to supplied cumulative gross amounts. A key is the 32-byte hash of its Actor document, read opaquely; each amount is a 32-byte unsigned whole-number count of the burnt asset's minimal units, in big-endian order, not a digest or residual balance. The selected network fixes the unit, which is not a frame member; no fractional scale or rounding enters this gross amount. The map is a subject document with a variable number of entries. Every funded Actor remains, including one with no current act or residual balance; canonical order and unique keys give one entry per name. Static arithmetic does not open Actor documents or establish destruction. The oracle opens them, enforces one accepted name per token at a time within the network and maps raw burn asset identifiers to these names before comparing lineage-derived gross amounts under (`def:lineage:gross`). Missing evidence is not zero. The exact domain, map encoding and candidate document identity are designed under (`amend:identity:frame-document-design`); registry publication, completed identity admission, implementation and application of the selected chain premises remain (`open:identity:frame-document-admission`).

**Rule (Closure frame checking)** · `rule:actepoch:frame-checking` Check the supplied frame and its Actor domain against entering gross totals and ledger state, then check nonnegative gross progression, current act charges, residual balances and cumulative counts with gross totals as exact integer counts in the network's fixed minimal unit under (`def:identity:frame-amount`); charges, residual balances and other burn-denominated quantities are exact values in that same network unit, possibly fractional, in their own representations, while prices keep their own denominations. A missing required entering entry is not implicit zero; a new Actor uses the explicit initial state. The outgoing gross totals equal the frame and outgoing ledger equals the calculated transition. These are relations among supplied facts and computed values, without opening an Actor name. History closure compares successive semantic coordinates; chain recognition and the asset-to-name relation belong to (`rule:actepoch:oracle-checking`).

**Definition (Epoch root)** · `def:actepoch:result` The epoch root identifies the complete static set. The retained result slug names this root, not a computed epoch result. Its three 32-byte references, in order, are epoch document, entering-state document and selected-input/evidence document. The name does not make computed outputs another committed branch. All required referenced calculation documents belong to that set; there is no floor-copy slot, marker, parent or external interpretation locator. An oracle marker may accept this root under (`def:lineage:accepted-epoch`), without any reverse reference. Optional computed offerings remain outside it. Its representation is designed by (`schema:identity:common-result`), with exact-byte binding admitted as design for pure static operations under (`amend:identity:common-static-admission`) and (`def:identity:common-document-recipes`); the test namespace is assigned by (`schema:identity:common-document-parameters`), while registry evidence and coordinated application enablement remain (`open:identity:common-document-admission`).

**Definition (Core commitment set)** · `def:actepoch:core-set` The core commitment set consists of authored and supplied calculation inputs, sufficient cumulative entering facts and irreducible selections or observations, with their required documents. The root’s three branches select this complete static set. It contains act approvals under recorded keys and supplied gross amounts keyed by opaque Actor names, but no Actor document, asset-to-name table, authorized-key relation, key history, retirement, marker or predecessor. Closing values and optional computed copies are outside the root. The only outward denotation is the Actor name, whose meaning is opened by the oracle; static calculation does not follow it. Fixed public specification semantics are the checker’s interpretation, not a retrieval dependency.

**Definition (Closure record and publication association)** · `def:actepoch:closure-record` The closure record is the complete static commitment set read by one epoch’s calculation. It coincides with (`def:actepoch:core-set`), rather than excluding an opaque marker field from a larger publication carrier. network-v0.37.1 ``def:network:static-commitment-set`` and ``def:epoch:document-set-commitment`` (closure) fix this input boundary. Markers and their root references stand beside it on the oracle layer under (`rule:lineage:commitments`); the marker carries the literal root under (`def:devnet:wire-marker`), the root's recipe and exact bytes remain (`open:identity:chain-marker-admission`), and the dependency analysis remains (`open:lineage:marker-placement`).

**Definition (Carried state)** · `def:actepoch:carried-state` Carried state supplies the accumulated facts needed to validate the subjects this epoch touches. It includes sufficient entering ledger, semantic, title, settlement, schedule, spent-slot and placement state, without a predecessor identity or an epoch number. Title facts supply the shared completed-state graph, handshake completion and counted-reference dispositions, and pending and attached challenges under (`def:actepoch:title-entering`); schedule facts state what is in force and pending. The current calculation derives outgoing values. History closure compares those values to the next supplied entering facts. Neither contains an Actor-to-authorized-key relation. Concrete sufficient projections and origin interpretation remain (`open:actepoch:state-provenance`); touched-subject projections must retain any domain-wide totals a check consumes and are not permission to omit required support.

The completing Ratify provides the anchor by which a later Accept cites that state; it is not a second independent state identity. A state is spent when it has a child, but spent does not mean unavailable: another completed handshake may add another child to it. Parent-state facts form a finite rooted tree represented by shared prefixes, not an exclusive-owner map or a consumed-candidate set. network-v0.37.1 ``def:graph:settlement-parties``, ``def:graph:settlement-structure`` and ``thm:graph:epoch-branch-fold`` (closure) supply this meaning.

**Definition (Transfer)** · `def:actepoch:title-transfer` A transfer is one completed handshake identified by its Accept. It retains the Bid, the Accept's single counted spent-state anchor, the selected completing Ratify, the matching seller and buyer, and their checked structural relations. The seller is the Accept author and the terminal Actor of the cited state; the buyer is the Bid author; Accept targets buyer and Ratify targets seller. Bid's Offer identifies the Item but does not identify a spent state. Only the specified kind of asserted parent supplies the anchor. A sibling transfer neither wins over nor consumes this transfer. These are recorded-party predicates, not a finding of signing authority or external ownership. The corresponding public act subjects already own the authored fields; the transfer is a calculated fact, not a new act type. network-v0.37.1 ``def:graph:settlement-parties`` and ``def:graph:settlement-structure`` are closure statements.

**Rule (Completion selection)** · `rule:actepoch:title-completion` One Accept produces at most one child. If the entering facts already give its completing Ratify, later Ratifys do not replace it. Otherwise, from the correct Ratifys completing in this epoch, select the least act identifier in the paper's bytewise order. Resolve counted causal dependencies against the complete current group, not an incidental traversal order. The selection is evidence for this handshake only; every structurally completed competing Accept remains a separate child. Further Ratifys remain accepted, charged and counted acts with their own cells at self. This is network-v0.37.1 ``def:graph:settlement-structure`` and ``rem:graph:title-order-irrelevance`` (closure); the identifier comparison uses the admitted act identity, without assigning an encoding here.

**Definition (Timeline)** · `def:actepoch:title-timeline` A timeline is a genesis-rooted path of completed Accept identities. Equality is equality of genesis and path, not equality of terminal Actor, number of transfers, description or digest alone. The empty path ends at the genesis Actor. A nonempty path ends at the last transfer's buyer. A branch can be named without choosing it as the branch anyone honours. network-v0.37.1 ``def:graph:item-timeline-branch`` (closure) defines a computed incremental digest and treats a collision as a malformed summary; the corresponding path encoding and digest admission remain owed, and no recipe is assigned by this concept.

**Definition (Challenge facts)** · `def:actepoch:title-challenges` A buyer Withdraw is retained under its Bid and challenges every completed handshake under that Bid; a Rescind is retained under Bid and its author and challenges only handshakes whose Accept has that author. Retain each distinct cancellation act and the public fields needed to test the relation. A cancellation can be pending before an edge exists and attached to several edges once they exist; attachment does not consume the pending evidence needed for future matches. A wrong-party cancellation has no effect on an unmatched edge but remains its own accepted and charged act. The undated attachment identifies the cancellation and edge. No challenge deletes or renames an edge or makes a state unavailable. Its writing epoch and the paper's epoch-stamped grouping are obtained from the declared run when evaluating a timeline, not copied into title state. This implements the conceptual separation of network-v0.37.1 ``def:graph:branch-challenge`` and ``rem:graph:continuing-cancellation`` (closure) from the repository's static inputs. A challenge attaches by Accept identifier and cancellation act identifier; the writing epoch is attached evidence for the computed summary and is not part of that key.

**Convention (Document placement)** · `conv:actepoch:title-documents` The title-state projection is one subject of entering state, not a new common root. It may directly contain the map and graph that express that subject. Public authored parts with their own retrieval or retention subject remain separate documents and must resolve inside the static set when calculation needs them. State nodes, edges and pending-match facts do not each require a document merely because they have identities. The roles follow (`def:actepoch:document-grade`), (`inv:actepoch:part-separation`) and (`inv:actepoch:independent-members`); the representation design decides references and encodings without adding redundant common members. No certificate, terminal selection or computed branch digest becomes another reference of the epoch root.

**Definition (Calculation domain)** · `def:actepoch:title-domain` An Item is touched for this calculation when a current act's title or description predicate reads it, or when a cumulative calculation reads its settlement events. The required domain includes all Item, Offer, Bid, Accept, Ratify, cancellation and public-parent support on which those predicates depend. It is not limited to the Items printed as endpoints of this epoch's new acts. In particular, every epoch's standing reads the persistent recipient events of every completed handshake; the entering state carries them, in title state or the standing basis as the entering-state design decides, and sparse touching applies to graph and challenge facts. network-v0.37.1 ``post:epoch:standing-epoch-set-determinism``, ``post:epoch:standing-memorylessness`` and ``prop:scalability:allocation-input-maintenance-cost`` (closure) require the cumulative event census; a title-state projection limited to newly transferred Items cannot supply standing. Required public support is supplied within the set; an unresolved required operand prevents confirmation rather than inviting a history fetch.

**Definition (Entering title facts)** · `def:actepoch:title-entering` For each Item in that domain, entering state supplies the following semantic facts, directly or through public subjects resolvable inside the same static set. They contain no epoch number, boundary ordinal, movement epoch or cancellation-writing epoch.

| Facts | Exact purpose | Why retained |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Genesis present/absent, identity, completed status, Actor and public incidence | Establish root, Item identity and anchor type | Absence is a supplied premise of isolated checking, established historically only by a checked run |
| Complete shared parent-state graph, edge Accept/Bid and selected completing Ratify identities, parties and their public matching terms | Resolve every eligible anchor and derive terminal Actor | Interior states remain usable; keeping only leaves or a current owner loses legal future spends |
| Prior accepted/countable domain and required public reference terms | Check membership, exact targets, author/target equality and counted-parent support | A prior object reference alone is not evidence of its earlier eligibility |

| Each relevant handshake's completion status and selected completing Ratify, if any | Select a first completion only once and identify later repetitions | First completion is a fact; no stored ordinal is needed to prefer it over a current candidate |

**Rule (Prior facts and local proof)** · `rule:actepoch:title-prior-facts` Validate internal consistency of the supplied facts, including unique genesis, one parent state and at most one child per Accept, coherent selected Ratify/parties, closed references, acyclicity and challenge matching. Treat the supplied prior completion and counted-reference dispositions as entering premises, not as proofs that they occurred in history. A current reference to an earlier act reads its required public terms from this set and its prior disposition from entering state. Current membership, equality and causal layering are calculated afresh. The declared-run check later establishes that the prior premises equal previously computed facts; an isolated static verdict does not. This is the use of (`inv:actepoch:static-closure`) and (`rule:actepoch:parent-connection`), not permission to trust a supplied current result instead of calculating it.

**Rule (Current title transition)** · `rule:actepoch:title-transition` Check current accepted acts and their counted parent relations under the fixed specification, using supplied prior facts for earlier operands. For each current Accept, record the anchor/reference disposition at that point; an anchor must already be complete or precede the Accept in the current counted causal layers. Check Bid–Accept–Ratify reference order, party matching and cycles over the complete current counted domain. A forward reference is not repaired later. Resolve first completions by (`rule:actepoch:title-completion`), add all resulting children, and retain every cancellation for all its matching edges. Keep malformed or incomplete handshakes on self-retention; do not convert their failure into rejection of otherwise accepted and charged acts. network-v0.37.1 ``itm:graph:settlement-structure-well-formedness``, ``rem:graph:settlement-party-mismatch`` and ``thm:graph:epoch-branch-fold`` (closure) govern these distinctions. A concrete algorithm must demonstrate agreement across causal layers and co-epochal ties before implementation is admitted.

**Proposition (Undated transition sufficiency)** · `prop:actepoch:title-sufficiency` Conditional on internally coherent entering facts with the dispositions above, the undated title transition for a complete current accepted group is determined without an earlier-epoch read or an epoch number. Proof: every structural predicate reads identity, accepted/countable membership, public fields, a completed parent state or a prior checked disposition. Earlier-versus-current membership distinguishes earlier completion; counted causal layers settle current precedence. Prior completion prevents a later replacement, while the specified minimum among eligible current Ratifys settles a current tie. Child construction depends on these predicates and not challenge dates. Challenge matching is identity/author matching, and retention preserves all future matches. The outgoing graph and matching facts are consequently determined by those operands. This proves sufficiency for the undated transition only: it does not infer historical correctness of the entering facts, dated challenges, a complete certificate or a winning branch. The proof's obligations are the concrete semantic projection and independent transition controls; no executed claim follows.

**Example (A later referent does not repair a forward citation)** · `ex:actepoch:title-forward` If an earlier Accept's anchor was not yet completed, supplying that anchor in this epoch does not make the Accept eligible: its earlier checked disposition remains unsupported. A fresh eligible Accept can cite the now-completed state. Likewise a Ratify that preceded its Accept is not reconsidered as a first completion merely because the Accept now exists.

**Example (Two children and a later challenge)** · `ex:actepoch:title-siblings` Two eligible Accepts spending one completed state, each with a completing Ratify, add two children. A later matching cancellation annotates both where its buyer/seller relation matches and propagates along their descendant paths; it removes neither child, changes neither identity nor previously selected completing Ratify, and changes no outward recipient under the explicit persistent-recipient rule.

**Rule (Growth and retention)** · `rule:actepoch:title-growth` Retain all completed states and edges and all relevant pending and attached evidence; an interior state cannot be dropped on the ground that it has been spent. network-v0.37.1 ``thm:graph:epoch-branch-fold`` (closure) states: “Therefore an Item with genesis has exactly one more state node than completed transfers, regardless of branching; only the fan-out of a state is unbounded.” Its proof also states: “This node bound does not bound replay or path-enumeration work, whose growth must be measured and disclosed separately from the one-cell-per-act allocation pass.” The graph is finite for finite input. Distinct cancellations and pending handshakes grow with distinct accepted acts; no constant cap, expiry or safe pruning rule is inferred. Shared prefixes avoid copying an ancestor for every descendant; they do not justify a bound on total state size or service work. Resource limits and partial-read policies must preserve a truthful unavailable result rather than silently omit branches.

**Definition (Outgoing title facts)** · `def:actepoch:title-outgoing` The transition computes the updated undated graph, new and retained handshake dispositions, selected completing evidence, cancellation facts and matches, and the applicable ordinary revision facts. It also supplies persistent settlement event inputs to the standing calculation. These outgoing values are calculated results under (`rule:actepoch:computed-publication`), not a second committed branch; a subsequent epoch supplies the necessary projection as its entering state. No global owner, winning edge, consumed-candidate set or stored completion epoch is an output of this concept.

**Rule (Title seam)** · `rule:actepoch:title-seam` For an already statically validated declared run, compare the next entering title facts with the semantic projection of previously computed outgoing facts for every Item and support subject the next calculation reads. Compare genesis, all relevant graph edges and their parent/evidence identities, checked reference dispositions, pending/completed handshakes, selected Ratify, cancellation identities and matches, and required revision facts; compare absence as well as presence. Derivable caches need not be independently compared when the representation excludes them. The run checker retains a calculated accumulated map across epochs that do not touch an Item, so that an untouched interval does not make an old Item look absent. An empty-origin run establishes initial absence; an unanchored interval leaves its initial register conditional. Persistent recipient events remain a cumulative comparison domain at every epoch, even when graph or challenge projections are sparse. The register is a checker result, not a new common document or an epoch-number member of state. This refines the title coordinate of (`rule:actepoch:parent-connection`) without rerunning formation or selecting an authoritative run.

**Definition (Dated title read)** · `def:actepoch:title-read` A title read names a declared run, an evaluation point in it and a genesis-rooted timeline. It derives act writing/completion locations from membership in that run, selects the graph prefix at that point and follows the named path. If the path has not completed there, it has no title there. It returns the terminal Actor and applicable challenge evidence without selecting another path on the caller's behalf. Reading earlier points from only the final undated state is insufficient: the run, or equivalent admitted evidence of its relevant partition, must be supplied. Missing run evidence leaves the dated read unavailable and does not weaken the local static verdict.

**Definition (Computed title certificate)** · `def:actepoch:title-summary` The certificate summarizes the graph for the named run prefix, its paths and terminal Actors, pending challenges and epoch-stamped edge challenges. In that computation, cancellation identities are associated with their writing epochs from the run, and attachments can be grouped as network-v0.37.1 ``def:graph:branch-challenge`` (closure) specifies. The certificate is neither entering state nor an additional output commitment. An optional published copy is a vendor-named courtesy under (`rule:actepoch:computed-publication`); it has no authority in acceptance and no conformance claimed by merely being served. The calculation and an optional-copy comparison have distinct obligations.

**Argument (Date correspondence and its limit)** · `arg:actepoch:title-dates` network-v0.37.1 ``def:graph:branch-challenge`` (closure) says, “Both are carried as pending evidence, with cancellation act identifier and writing epoch, until a matching edge exists; later matching edges receive all pending matches.” Its full fold, network-v0.37.1 ``thm:graph:epoch-branch-fold`` (closure), consumes “its complete epoch partition”. The repository's entering document is the sufficient undated projection of those facts for one transition, not a claim to contain the paper's entire dated fold result. The run supplies the omitted coordinate when the full result is computed. No date is needed to decide child existence, match a cancellation to an edge, preserve its availability for a future edge or retain a completion event. A date is needed to report when a challenge begins and what title was at a named earlier point; those are expressly run computations. This is why removing dates from state does not remove dates from the certificate or claim that two differently partitioned histories have identical certificates.

**Rule (Origin and ordered scope)** · `rule:actepoch:title-origin` Empty initial title facts contain no genesis, branch or challenge. The local checker can check that supplied emptiness is internally consistent but cannot establish that the epoch was the first of a real history. The declared-run checker establishes the empty boundary and its own numbering; the dated title reader refuses an act placed at boundary zero under network-v0.37.1 ``def:graph:title-certificate`` and ``def:graph:authoritative-act-order`` (closure). The existing sentence that the ordinal-zero title condition is not claimed by the counter-free static form remains true; its concrete run/certificate comparison is designed here, not declared executed or added to every static check.

**Rule (No implicit owner selection)** · `rule:actepoch:title-consumers` A predicate that needs a state reads that cited state; a read that needs title names a timeline and evaluation point. Neither the record nor replay chooses a winner between completed branches. Ordinary authored description, common recipients, optional display and external honouring remain different subjects. No owner map is retained as a compatibility input for the new calculation.

**Remark (Standing predicates retained)** · `rem:actepoch:title-standing` network-v0.37.1 ``cond:comparator:faithfulness-title`` (closure) permits counterparty allocation only on structural completion under the common branch fold and excludes both later challenge and terminal selection as recipient-changing inputs. network-v0.37.1 ``def:epoch:effective-act-contribution`` and ``def:epoch:standing-recipient-resolution`` (closure) still require the applicable positive-coordinate and anchored-recipient predicates. The removal concerns the single-owner conflict/consumption rule and cancellation veto, not those independent predicates. No recipient is inferred merely from being a terminal Actor on a chosen path; the event fixes its recorded counterparty. Shared ancestors and a common Bid are not charged once per path. At network-v0.37.1, ``lem:epoch:authored-vouch-withdrawable``, ``post:epoch:settlement-standing-title-coupling``, ``def:epoch:effective-act-contribution`` and ``def:epoch:standing-recipient-resolution`` (closure) give one persistent settlement rule: settlement singleton cells have no netting or other withdrawal route; once outward, allocation persists in current and future cumulative standing. A later Withdraw or Rescind is pending or attached challenge evidence and re-weighs nothing home. Withdrawability covers the stance-folded Opinion, Reference and Invitation families only. The full recipient scope is designed and unbuilt and belongs to the coordinated replacement cut; no settlement tail is excluded or held for paper interpretation.

**Convention (One successor and historical bytes)** · `conv:actepoch:title-removal` Remove the single-owner design from current normative authority and replace its implementation and controls. Retain an obsolete implementation label only as the factual description of still-present code pending its scheduled removal, not as a second normative or historical contract. Retire that label with its last code/registry caller in the atomic replacement cut. Git history and the meaning of already published bytes are not a second current design. Under (`metathm:labels:warrant-lapse`) and (`amend:labels:pre-one`), retire or rename old authored labels whose subject disappears and update every citation in the deciding commit; no retired-label alias scheme is owed. A changed implementation must not silently change an admitted carrier or give an old claim new meaning. (`gate:identity:implementation`), (`req:identity:admission-record`) and (`rule:identity:recipe-permanence`) govern successor representation and historical bytes. The single-owner calculation and every dependent operation are replaced together after successor admission; delivery of title-bearing documents published under that design is withdrawn in the same cut, without opaque historical carriage. Earlier bytes keep their meaning wherever held. No current service offering is withdrawn by this documents design.

**Verification (Replacement evidence)** · `verif:actepoch:title-transition` Planned evidence, not an executed control: compare an independent full-run branch calculation with repeated undated transitions and semantic seam comparisons; include siblings, reuse of an interior spent state, same-epoch counted anchors and cycles, multiple correct Ratifys with the specified tie break, forward Accept and Ratify references never revived, absent genesis, distinct repeated cancellations before/with/after completion, seller-specific matches, buyer-wide matches, future-edge attachment, unchanged outward event recipients, sparse touched domains, missing public support, unanchored runs, empty origin and boundary-zero certificate refusal. Verify that multiple paths do not multiply cells or duplicate ancestor charges and that ordinary Owner updates create no transfer. **Rule (Title claim boundary)** · `rule:actepoch:title-claims` The document describes a designed successor under (`rule:testing:public-record-conformance`); no existing control realizes it by a new citation.
