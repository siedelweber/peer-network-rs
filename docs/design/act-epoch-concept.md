# Documents of acts and epochs · `spec:actepoch:concept`

**Table (Documents, layers and verdicts)** · `tab:actepoch:overview` The static verdict says “The epoch as given is internally consistent” and includes completed checks and unavailable private checks; the history verdict says “The declared run is accounting-consistent across its checked seams” and reports whether the empty initial boundary was checked. The first reads one static set under its fixed public specification; the second compares computed states of already validated epochs in a supplied order. Neither establishes chain truth or signing authority. The separate oracle assesses those subjects under (`spec:lineage:concept`). Common documents have fixed counts, order and 32-byte content members; subject documents use their plain shapes. The Actor document is outside the epoch; its opaque hash alone names the Actor inside it. All successor subjects are designed and unbuilt.

| Document / layer | Members and role | Refers to / checks |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Actor — subject, outside the set | Asset identifier and 32-byte blinder, namespace and version | Hash names the Actor; oracle opens it, checks prefix and one accepted document per token at a time |
| Act — common | Author name, sequence, type, typed part, parents, content, dependencies | Seven 32-byte members; no retirement or host member |
| Witnessed act — common | Act hash, signing key, signature half one, half two | Four 32-byte members; complete-act approval under the recorded key |
| Typed and public parts | Type coordinates, incidence, optional license, settlement and public parents | Nineteen act types; required public documents inside the set |
| Removable parts | Act identity, client randomness, content or dependencies | Commitments stay; private openings are not required public inputs |
| Acts | Ordered pairs of act hash and witnessed-act hash | Pair equality; containment supplies completion, position is place |
| Epoch — common | Acts, frame | Two references; no counter, predecessor, marker, signature or seal |
| Frame — subject | Actor-document hash to cumulative gross amount | Keys and amounts 32 bytes; gross is unsigned big-endian minimal-unit count; no Actor document, unit member or authority map |
| Entering state and selected evidence | Cumulative facts for touched subjects, including undated branch, settlement and challenge facts, and irreducible inputs | Derive outgoing values; no stored epoch number |
| Epoch root — common | Epoch, entering state, selected inputs/evidence | Three references; complete static set, no computed-copy branch |
| Static and history closure | Internal validity / declared-run accounting consistency | History receives validated epochs and their computed states; no parent link |
| Oracle and optional displays | Accepted roots, authority and chain gross / computed copies | Oracle marker points inward; courtesy copies outside root, consumer/name mapping held |

## Purpose · `sec:actepoch:purpose`

**Formulation (Purpose)** · `formul:actepoch:purpose` A holder can check one epoch without fetching its past or relying on a host service. Static closure establishes internal validity of its supplied set; a separate declared run permits accounting comparisons across seams. Oracle recognition establishes neither verdict by itself. Authored inputs, cumulative entering facts, independent observations and computed outputs have distinct subjects. This extends (`model:design:epoch-query-publication`) within (`rec:xchg:concept-record`).

**Convention (Historical boundary)** · `conv:actepoch:history` This decided and unbuilt concept concerns successor documents. Historical publication bytes, assigned theories, recipes and controls retain their meanings under (`inv:api:document-bytes`) and (`rule:identity:recipe-permanence`). Removing a member from a successor does not remove it from an admitted object. Paper references are to network-v0.37.4. References in the lineage area name statements of the separate lineage concept, (`spec:lineage:concept`), rather than declarations minted here; their direction is used without claiming its successor realization implemented. Current local policies and conditional claims remain under (`dec:devnet:author-authority-case`), (`amend:devnet:marker-settlement`) and (`rule:testing:public-record-conformance`).

## Principles · `sec:actepoch:principles`

**Invariant (Static closure)** · `inv:actepoch:static-closure` A static check reads only the epoch root’s complete committed input set under the identified fixed public specification; it reads no earlier epoch, external history or current host service. Its verdict is internal consistency of the supplied data, with completed checks and unavailable private checks explicitly listed. Missing required public input prevents confirmation. Every earlier-state operand needed by this calculation is supplied as cumulative entering state. The argument is that a verdict restricted to the data read cannot establish a historical fact, while supplied state permits reproducible calculation after earlier private material is removed. This eliminates traversal, not possible state growth. History accounting and oracle assessment have separate inputs and verdicts under (`rule:actepoch:parent-connection`) and (`rule:actepoch:oracle-checking`).

**Invariant (Independent common members)** · `inv:actepoch:independent-members` A common document has no semantic member that is null, constant or determined by another member of that document. The common documents are the act, witnessed act, epoch and epoch root under (`def:actepoch:document-grade`). Every reader of the corresponding act or epoch handles these roots, so a single shape makes their comparison and interpretation uniform without duplicate descriptions that can disagree. Sub-documents instead state their subject directly: an optional member, integer, ratio or list is permitted where forcing uniformity would add a document or type without adding a fact. Envelope discriminants and binding representation are not semantic members under (`def:actepoch:subject-membership`); their interchange rules remain distinct.

**Definition (Common and subject documents)** · `def:actepoch:document-grade` Common documents are the shared roots through which every act or epoch is identified and related: act, witnessed act, epoch and epoch root. A later document is common when interpreting every instance of the corresponding act or epoch requires its root regardless of the subject a reader selects; a document reached to inspect one particular subject is a sub-document. This is a role test, not a count of clients or requests: being required somewhere in complete replay does not make an evidence part common. Common roots keep (`inv:actepoch:independent-members`) and (`conv:actepoch:fixed-width`) strictly, because their shape is paid for everywhere. Typed acts, public and removable parts, result groups and evidence parts use their subject's plain shape, avoiding extra types or references that add no fact.

**Definition (Subject membership)** · `def:actepoch:subject-membership` A semantic member is a fact whose change changes the subject the document describes. Envelope framing and private random values bind the interpretation or opening without each being a separate semantic member. This distinction separates what a document says from all the bytes a witness must cover.

**Rule (Membership and witness coverage)** · `rule:actepoch:subject-membership` Place a semantic member in a document exactly when changing it changes that document's subject. Apply the independent-member and document-grade rules to its form. A witness covers the exact document bytes, including its envelope and any private random value: omitting binding representation would permit the same apparent statement to be read or opened differently.

**Convention (Common content shape)** · `conv:actepoch:fixed-width` Every content member of a common document is 32 bytes, with fixed count and order: act seven, witnessed act four, epoch two and epoch root three. These widths are the decided form, so readers check one shape before opening its references; no common member is optional or variable. Keys, signature halves and sequences do not become hashes merely by having that width. Subject documents use their plain shapes. The envelope follows interchange, including (`inv:interchange:patch-identity`); no fixed total encoded length is claimed.

**Invariant (Independent parts)** · `inv:actepoch:part-separation` A common document refers by hash to its lists, maps and removable parts as separate documents. Each removable part and each public part with its own retrieval or retention subject has its own document. Public permanent calculation parts carry no random value; removable parts carry independent private random values generated by their author’s client, and their references survive removal. This distinguishes a removed opening from an empty one. The Actor naming document outside the static set has separate public prefix-search material under (`def:actepoch:actor-document`), not concealment of a calculation part. A subject document may contain the list or map directly expressing its subject under (`def:actepoch:document-grade`).

**Invariant (Type placement)** · `inv:actepoch:type-placement` A member whose presence or shape depends on act type belongs to the typed document; the common document names the type and refers to that document. The reason is that readers must see the structural distinction in the type rather than guess it from validation success. Interpretation and relational validity still need their owning rules; a hash reference alone establishes neither endpoint validity nor authority.

**Invariant (No extension member)** · `inv:actepoch:no-extension` A document has no opaque extension member. New meaning is given a further admitted version, under (`inv:interchange:minor-inclusion`) and (`law:interchange:major-boundary`). The reason is that an unclassified byte bag supplies no shared interpretation for a witnessed assertion. Removable authored content is a defined subject and is not such an extension. The designed successor version admits no extension bytes: an act bearing them is outside that version and must be refused as unadmitted, with no conformance claimed for that case under (`rule:testing:public-record-conformance`).

**Invariant (Witness separation)** · `inv:actepoch:witness-separation` A signature is held in a witness document beside its subject, never inside the document it signs. The reason is that authored bytes remain comparable independently of possession of a witness or the scheme used to witness them. Replacing or withholding a copy of a witness does not replace the historical witness selected by an accepted epoch. Separation of the approval document does not omit the approval projection from the complete recorded association.

**Rule (Core membership and retained copies)** · `rule:actepoch:computed-publication` The core commits authored and supplied calculation inputs, L cumulative entering state needed to avoid earlier-epoch reads, and V irreducible selections or observations required for the calculation. Closing values are computed outputs, not independent operands or required published copies. A subsequent epoch can supply those values as its entering state; history closure compares the semantic values. A published copy of a computed result is kept where a consumer reads it, as an optional public-interface answer under a vendor-namespaced name and as a courtesy. It may refer inward but is no member of a common document or the root, carries no authority in acceptance and has no conformance claimed under (`rule:testing:public-record-conformance`). A copy adds convenience rather than evidence because its value follows from the root; a vendor-namespaced answer can be served, changed or withdrawn without changing a document’s shape or a repository claim. The floor of network-v0.37.4 is this static set with the public calculation source, under ``def:deployment:published-epoch-replay-floor`` (closure); there is no independent F-only branch. realizes{``def:deployment:published-epoch-replay-floor``; This designs complete public static input carriage and separates optional computed copies; it asserts no implemented successor or oracle truth.}

**Invariant (Host-free authorship)** · `inv:actepoch:host-free-authorship` The act and its Actor witness contain nothing contributed by a host. The author forms the commitments and approves the complete act; the epoch has neither signature nor seal. Host service evidence has a separate subject, so it can vary without changing the authored statement or static root. Historical local constructions retain their admissions under (`conv:actepoch:history`); successor approval admission remains (`open:actepoch:host-association`).

## Act documents · `sec:actepoch:act`

**Definition (Author and type)** · `def:actepoch:author-type` An author name is the hash of the Actor document under (`dec:identity:actor-document-name`). Acts and frame keys use that same opaque 32-byte name under (`rule:identity:actor-document-hiding`); the oracle's explicit asset binding is supplied by the separately published Actor document. A type-specification document states the admitted type’s meaning and members independently of any act instance and is present in the static set. Its name belongs to a closed admitted roster: fetching a specification cannot admit it. Type specifications and assigned interchange theories have distinct roles.

**Definition (Actor naming document)** · `def:actepoch:actor-document` An Actor document binds a network’s name for an Actor to its single-unit asset. Its members are the chain-derived 32-byte asset identifier and a 32-byte public blinder, with the document’s full namespace and version, under (`dec:identity:actor-document-name`). It is a subject document, not a common root: it is opened for the naming subject and is never a required static input. It has no chain payload byte budget and never goes on the chain. The Actor varies the blinder until the document hash has the three-byte prefix assigned to its namespace and version; the blinder binds that representation without becoming another semantic fact about the asset. Prefix validity is checked when the document is opened, not by the epoch’s static checker. The Actor supplies the document to its host for publication under (`dec:identity:actor-document-name`): exact bytes retrievable by name from the announced oracle-side interface before external successor use, not merely retained privately or described by a schema. A reader opens those published bytes and checks the prefix and name, while current one-document-per-token acceptance still requires the oracle association evidence; an interface answer alone establishes no authority or currentness. Within one network the oracle accepts one Actor document for a token at any time and uses that document to relate a burn's raw asset identifier to a frame's Actor name. It refuses a second document opening to the same asset unless the document arrives through a succession flow the oracle admits; such a flow replaces the accepted document and retains the earlier name beside the new under (`amend:identity:actor-document-succession`). No such flow is designed or admitted, so every second document is refused until (`open:identity:actor-document-succession`) is discharged. A later version of what defines an Actor changes its document and prefix, so its names can be distinguished and sorted without being opened. No Actor document, asset-to-name table or key-authority map belongs to the static set. The representation is designed in (`amend:identity:actor-document-design`) and pure comparison is admitted as design by (`amend:identity:actor-static-admission`); the test namespace is assigned in (`schema:identity:actor-document`), production allocation remains (`open:identity:actor-document-namespaces`), and registry evidence, honest generation and application enablement remain (`open:identity:actor-document-admission`) under (`req:identity:admission-record`). The pure operations remain unbuilt and establish neither candidate-source provenance nor oracle association.

**Convention (Actor-name prefix assignments)** · `conv:actepoch:actor-prefix` The Actor-document design records the table from three-byte prefix to namespace and version. Its first test assignment is 00 00 00 for org.peer-network.test.actor at major 1 and minor 0; 00 00 01 is reserved for the first real-network version without namespace text or a production theory. This table is absent from epoch documents. Static checking treats every Actor name as opaque, without testing its prefix; opening and uniqueness checks belong to the oracle and the Actor-document admission. The test namespace and concrete theory design stand in (`schema:identity:actor-document`); production allocation remains (`open:identity:actor-document-namespaces`), and neither prefix is an implemented identity admission. The representation design records these as namespace/theory-coordinate rows shared across patch values under (`tab:identity:actor-prefixes`); patch does not define another theory, while its bytes remain part of the document hash.

**Definition (Common act)** · `def:actepoch:act` The common act identifies what the Actor authored and points to its parts. Its seven 32-byte members, in order, are author name, sequence, act type, typed-document reference, parents-document reference, content-document reference and dependency-document reference. The author name is (`def:actepoch:actor-document`)’s hash, not a chain asset or key. Sequence identifies an author-local slot; the act identifier is the author/sequence/type triple, while its document hash names one exact realization. No retirement point, host or external run locator is carried. The oracle assesses the recorded key under (`rule:lineage:authority`). The envelope is not one of the seven. These successor meanings do not reinterpret admitted signed carriers under (`open:actepoch:authority-boundary`). Its representation is designed by (`schema:identity:common-act`), with exact-byte binding admitted as design for pure static operations under (`amend:identity:common-static-admission`) and (`def:identity:common-document-recipes`); the test namespace is assigned by (`schema:identity:common-document-parameters`), while registry evidence and coordinated application enablement remain (`open:identity:common-document-admission`). The act document is the signing subject; the complete recorded act of network-v0.37.4 ``def:graph:act-projections`` (closure) is its resolved association with the paired witnessed act, including all eight semantic projections. The field named sequence supplies the act nonce and carries no ordering meaning. This deployment refuses a repeated author/sequence slot, a local restriction stronger than the paper’s author/nonce/family uniqueness; it is not claimed as the paper’s complete acceptance domain. No stored member, hash recipe or approval changes.

**Proposition (Publish identity)** · `prop:actepoch:publish-refinement` Publish remains one act type because its minting incidence already distinguishes the license-bearing case. Splitting it would refine the type coordinate of the act identifier under (`amend:identity:act-stops`) for a fact already given by incidence under (`alg:design:formation`). An optional member in the typed document expresses that fact under (`inv:actepoch:independent-members`) without imposing another identifier distinction on every reader. The license follows (`rule:actepoch:publish-license`); the shared author/sequence slot gains no second allocation space.

**Rule (Publish license presence)** · `rule:actepoch:publish-license` Publish's typed document holds an optional license member, present exactly when the act mints its Content and absent otherwise. Incidence determines that presence under (`alg:design:formation`); the license is the exact attribution/provenance degree pair retained across payload removal. This states the minting fact once without splitting Publish's identifier type or using a null placeholder.

**Definition (Typed acts)** · `def:actepoch:typed-acts` The type's document holds its varying semantic coordinates, legs reference and applicable license or settlement members. Ratios retain exact authored numerator and denominator under (`amend:identity:formation-three`); no unit-one or vintage-zero constants are carried as independent choices. Registration, Leave, Withdraw, Rescind and De-invite have no semantic coordinate. Publish and Owner have attachment; Opinion polarity/reaction; Affinity association/attraction; Join Request formality/urgency; Accept and Ratify comfort/equity; Participant continuity/interactivity/responsibility; Invitation formality/relevance/urgency; Tag confidence/relevance; Review and Reference effort/enthusiasm; Bid generosity/urgency; Send importance. Accept carries settles and Ratify carries settles and accepts as exact references with the successor’s cited-state structural-completion meaning; exact typed-subject admission remains owed. No layout member is stored when type determines it. The nineteen types retain the nineteen families without creating extra actions or independently debit-able legs; Publish's license follows (`rule:actepoch:publish-license`). realizes{``amend:identity:formation-three``; This uses exact authored coordinate pairs, not private entropy, custody or production selection.}

**Definition (Public parts)** · `def:actepoch:public-parts` A legs document states the incidence of the act's one binary leg or two ordered A/T projections; the shared middle and role relation are not independently contradictory fields. A parents document states the complete distinct asserted-parent list; settlement references contribute to the derived causal-parent relation without another stored union. A protocol document states a nonconstant public-reference list when an admitted type needs it. Current supported types require the protocol list empty, so they contain no redundant constant-empty protocol reference. Public list documents may be shared by exact content where no act-specific field is needed; the empty parents list is a real empty document, never a missing reference. These documents remain available to ordinary formation and retained structural replay. Asserted references and the counted parent relation are distinct: network-v0.37.4 ``def:graph:counted-parent-relation`` (closure) counts only a parent completed before its child, in an earlier epoch or an earlier current causal layer; a forward citation gains no counted edge from later completion.

**Definition (Removable parts)** · `def:actepoch:removable-parts` A content document holds the act identifier, its client's private random value and the authored content. A dependency document holds that identifier, an independent client random value and the complete dependency list. Empty content or a complete empty list remains an opening with its own binding representation, not absence. The two leaves cannot be shared between different act identifiers. Removal leaves the common document, its references and witness unchanged; a tombstone is holder state outside this document set. The membership criterion concerns the authored value; private randomness supplies concealment framing under (`def:actepoch:subject-membership`). No host generates or contributes these values.

**Definition (Witnessed act)** · `def:actepoch:witnessed-act` A witnessed act identifies the complete act and the key under which its approval verifies. Its four 32-byte members are act-document hash, public verification key and signature’s first and second halves, in that order; the act hash is the signed message. The key records who signed, not authorization for an Actor. Static checking verifies the signature under this key; oracle assessment tests it against the epoch’s delegate set under (`rule:lineage:authority`). A negative authority result does not rewrite the act or its calculation. There is no host, receipt, seal, retirement, position or time member. The complete-act message and recorded-key verification are admitted as pure-operation design by (`amend:identity:common-static-admission`); signing and complete subject binding remain held, and no successor signed-preparation or historical-role witness is required. Its representation is designed by (`schema:identity:common-witnessed-act`), with exact-byte binding admitted as design for pure static operations under (`amend:identity:common-static-admission`) and (`def:identity:common-document-recipes`); the test namespace is assigned by (`schema:identity:common-document-parameters`), while registry evidence and coordinated application enablement remain (`open:identity:common-document-admission`).

**Rule (Host realization and conformance)** · `rule:actepoch:host-conformance` At network-v0.37.4, ``def:graph:author-commitment-preparation``, ``def:graph:verified-act`` and ``def:graph:approval-witness`` (closure) describe author-formed commitments and one complete-act approval. The static floor, ``def:deployment:published-epoch-replay-floor`` (closure), requires no host entropy, seal or signed preparation. Exact successor approval and opening associations remain (`open:actepoch:host-association`). Historical proposal/header/seal/approval constructions retain their own meanings and local duties. Under (`rule:testing:public-record-conformance`), signature validity establishes neither custody, entropy nor key-to-Actor authority; the courtesy ``def:graph:courtesy-handshake`` supplies no closure conformance claim. realizes{``def:deployment:published-epoch-replay-floor``; This states the successor public-input duty without importing historical host constructions; exact successor approval admission remains held.}

## Epoch documents · `sec:actepoch:epoch`

**Definition (Public source and selected evidence)** · `def:actepoch:source-evidence` Fixed public specification semantics interpret the supplied calculation inputs. They are an explicit checker premise, not a mutable external locator in the epoch. A supplied object has the semantics fixed by its defining statement under network-v0.37.4 ``def:network:supplied-object`` (closure). Every selected version, constant, numerical point or observation not fixed by that specification is supplied inside the static set and bound to its subject. Required supplied values are asserted at the oracle layer, committed within the epoch, read as given and bound to their consuming subjects under ``post:network:supplied-value-custody`` (closure). Their assertion warrants are not calculation operands or closure predicates. Under ``def:network:defaulted-constant`` (closure), an issued defaulted figure is an illustration and no fallback: every epoch commits its target even when it equals that figure. Exact operand homes and bindings remain (`open:actepoch:source-selection`); no external-reference fallback is admitted. Derived consequences are not independent evidence. Oracle declarations, histories and warrantors are not calculation operands under network-v0.37.4 ``def:comparator:frame-inputs`` and ``post:deployment:replay-floor-scope`` (closure). The operand groups and binding direction are (`def:actepoch:evidence`); edition-fixed and epoch-specific selections are distinguished by (`rule:actepoch:parent-connection`). Exact interpretation-transition and operand-binding relations remain (`open:actepoch:source-selection`).

**Definition (Epoch)** · `def:actepoch:epoch` An epoch document names its accepted acts and supplied frame. Its two 32-byte members are acts-document reference and frame-document reference, in that order. It has no counter, ordinal, predecessor, marker, signature or seal. Position is place in the acts list and count is its length. The root of its own set names no external boundary witness under network-v0.37.4 ``def:epoch:document-set-commitment`` (closure). Markers name accepted roots from outside; the earlier carrier’s marker association remains that carrier’s admitted meaning under (`dec:design:epoch-record-order`). Act sequence slots are independent of epoch numbering. Its representation is designed by (`schema:identity:common-epoch`), with exact-byte binding admitted as design for pure static operations under (`amend:identity:common-static-admission`) and (`def:identity:common-document-recipes`); the test namespace is assigned by (`schema:identity:common-document-parameters`), while registry evidence and coordinated application enablement remain (`open:identity:common-document-admission`).

**Definition (Acts)** · `def:actepoch:acts` The acts document is the complete ordered list of accepted acts and witnesses in the host’s selected order. Each entry pairs the 32-byte act hash first with the 32-byte witnessed-act hash second; the latter’s act member must equal the first. One act occurs once and projections remain one action. Containment identifies completion and co-epochal membership; position is place, without a stored completion ordinal. Earlier placement facts needed by static validation are supplied in entering state. The empty list is a real document; chunking must preserve one finite complete authoritative order. Removing a private opening changes neither pair member. network-v0.37.4 ``def:graph:act-completion-epoch`` and ``def:graph:authoritative-act-order`` are closure statements; ordinal and boundary comparisons have the scoped treatment of (`rule:actepoch:parent-connection`). Its representation is designed by (`schema:identity:common-acts`), with exact-byte binding admitted as design for pure static operations under (`amend:identity:common-static-admission`) and (`def:identity:common-document-recipes`); the test namespace is assigned by (`schema:identity:common-document-parameters`), while registry evidence and coordinated application enablement remain (`open:identity:common-document-admission`). Required public settlement and anchor support resolves inside this static set; earlier counted-reference dispositions are entering facts under (`rule:actepoch:title-prior-facts`), not earlier-epoch locators.

**Definition (Frame)** · `def:actepoch:frame` The frame maps Actor names to supplied cumulative gross amounts. A key is the 32-byte hash of its Actor document, read opaquely; each amount is a 32-byte unsigned whole-number count of the burnt asset's minimal units, in big-endian order, not a digest or residual balance. The selected network fixes the unit, which is not a frame member; no fractional scale or rounding enters this gross amount. The map is a subject document with a variable number of entries. Every funded Actor remains, including one with no current act or residual balance; canonical order and unique keys give one entry per name. Static arithmetic does not open Actor documents or establish destruction. The oracle opens them, enforces one accepted name per token at a time within the network and maps raw burn asset identifiers to these names before comparing lineage-derived gross amounts under (`def:lineage:gross`). Missing evidence is not zero. The exact domain, map encoding, pure operations and document identity are admitted as design under (`amend:identity:frame-document-design`); theory publication and correspondence, independent vectors, controls, implementation, epoch use, oracle recognition and application of the selected chain premises remain (`open:identity:frame-document-admission`).

**Rule (Closure frame checking)** · `rule:actepoch:frame-checking` Check the supplied frame and its Actor domain against entering gross totals and ledger state, then check nonnegative gross progression, current act charges, residual balances and cumulative counts with gross totals as exact integer counts in the network's fixed minimal unit under (`def:identity:frame-amount`); charges, residual balances and other burn-denominated quantities are exact values in that same network unit, possibly fractional, in their own representations, while prices keep their own denominations. A missing required entering entry is not implicit zero; a new Actor uses the explicit initial state of (`def:actepoch:ledger-facts`), established by full ledger coverage and the full catalogue join. The required funded domain of (`rule:identity:frame-document-domain`) is statically every Actor of the entering ledger, every author in Q and every Actor with a positive frame entry; the frame’s semantic amount function reads each entry’s integer, an explicit zero as zero. The outgoing gross amount function equals that function on the required funded domain with initial-zero ledger rows omitted, and outgoing ledger equals (`rule:actepoch:ledger-transition`); a permitted explicit zero frame entry is not an independently stored zero ledger row. These are relations among supplied facts and computed values, without opening an Actor name. History closure compares successive semantic coordinates; chain recognition and the asset-to-name relation belong to (`rule:actepoch:oracle-checking`).

**Definition (Epoch root)** · `def:actepoch:result` The epoch root identifies the complete static set. The retained result slug names this root, not a computed epoch result. Its three 32-byte references, in order, are epoch document, entering-state document and selected-input/evidence document. The name does not make computed outputs another committed branch. All required referenced calculation documents belong to that set; there is no floor-copy slot, marker, parent or external interpretation locator. An oracle marker may accept this root under (`def:lineage:accepted-epoch`), without any reverse reference. Optional computed offerings remain outside it. Its representation is designed by (`schema:identity:common-result`), with exact-byte binding admitted as design for pure static operations under (`amend:identity:common-static-admission`) and (`def:identity:common-document-recipes`); the test namespace is assigned by (`schema:identity:common-document-parameters`), while registry evidence and coordinated application enablement remain (`open:identity:common-document-admission`).

**Definition (Core commitment set)** · `def:actepoch:core-set` The core commitment set consists of authored and supplied calculation inputs, sufficient cumulative entering facts and irreducible selections or observations, with their required documents. The root’s three branches select this complete static set. It contains act approvals under recorded keys and supplied gross amounts keyed by opaque Actor names, but no Actor document, asset-to-name table, authorized-key relation, key history, retirement, marker or predecessor. Closing values and optional computed copies are outside the root. The only outward denotation is the Actor name, whose meaning is opened by the oracle; static calculation does not follow it. Fixed public specification semantics are the checker’s interpretation, not a retrieval dependency.

**Definition (Closure record and publication association)** · `def:actepoch:closure-record` The closure record is the complete static commitment set read by one epoch’s calculation. It coincides with (`def:actepoch:core-set`), rather than excluding an opaque marker field from a larger publication carrier. network-v0.37.4 ``def:network:static-commitment-set`` and ``def:epoch:document-set-commitment`` (closure) fix this input boundary. Markers and their root references stand beside it on the oracle layer under (`rule:lineage:commitments`); the marker carries the literal root under (`def:devnet:wire-marker`), the root's representation and recipe are (`schema:identity:common-result`) and (`rule:identity:common-root`), with pure-operation design admission under (`amend:identity:common-static-admission`) and remaining implementation and enablement evidence under (`open:identity:common-document-admission`); lookup and compatibility remain (`open:identity:chain-marker-admission`), and the dependency analysis remains (`open:lineage:marker-placement`).

**Definition (Carried state)** · `def:actepoch:carried-state` Carried state supplies the accumulated facts needed to validate the subjects this epoch touches. It includes sufficient entering ledger, semantic, title, settlement, schedule, spent-slot and placement state, without a predecessor identity or an epoch number. **Definition (Compact ledger facts)** · `def:actepoch:ledger-facts` Designed and unbuilt, owned by ledger/epoch and records under (`open:actepoch:state-provenance`): the ledger map L is keyed by opaque Actor name and carries a pair (G_i, B_i), the previously consumed cumulative gross and entering residual balance. **Rule (Compact ledger transition)** · `rule:actepoch:ledger-transition` For the complete current proposed act set Q, let n_i count its distinct complete acts authored by i and G′_i be the frame’s supplied cumulative gross. With θ the positive in-force charged value, compute ΔG_i = G′_i − G_i, B′_i = B_i + ΔG_i − n_i θ and N′_i = N_i + n_i. Network-v0.37.4 ``def:epoch:evaluated-epoch-state``, ``eq:epoch:computed-residual-balance`` and ``eq:epoch:computed-action-count`` (closure) give these equations. The next controller output is not this epoch’s charge. Registration and a complete hyper-edge each count once; shared ancestors, projections and multiple paths add no charge. A new cancellation or repeated Ratify is a new paid act even when it adds no persistent event. An increment may be positive without an act, and an act may be charged with zero increment. No old act is repriced.

Refuse gross decrease, a missing required funded Actor’s frame entry, nonpositive current charge and a negative computed residual. The outgoing ledger carries the frame’s semantic gross and calculated B′ with the initial-zero normalization of (`def:actepoch:ledger-facts`); public support extends A by exactly the newly accepted Q, so its recovered count is N′. **Rule (Prior facts and local proof)** · `rule:actepoch:title-prior-facts` Validate internal consistency of the supplied facts, including unique genesis, one parent state and at most one child per Accept, coherent selected Ratify/parties, closed references, acyclicity and challenge matching. Treat the supplied prior completion and counted-reference dispositions as entering premises, not as proofs that they occurred in history. A current reference to an earlier act reads its required public terms from this set and its prior disposition from entering state. Current membership, equality and causal layering are calculated afresh. The declared-run check later establishes that the prior premises equal previously computed facts; an isolated static verdict does not. This is the use of (`inv:actepoch:static-closure`) and (`rule:actepoch:parent-connection`), not permission to trust a supplied current result instead of calculating it.

**Rule (Current title transition)** · `rule:actepoch:title-transition` Check current accepted acts and their counted parent relations under the fixed specification, using supplied prior facts for earlier operands. For each current Accept, record the anchor/reference disposition at that point; an anchor must be the completed genesis act or the selected completing Ratify of a structurally complete handshake on that Item, already complete or preceding the Accept in the current counted causal layers; a later Ratify repetition supplies no anchor. Check Bid–Accept–Ratify reference order, party matching and cycles over the complete current counted domain. A forward reference is not repaired later. Resolve first completions by (`rule:actepoch:title-completion`), add all resulting children, and retain every cancellation for all its matching edges. Keep malformed or incomplete handshakes on self-retention; do not convert their failure into rejection of otherwise accepted and charged acts. network-v0.37.4 ``itm:graph:settlement-structure-well-formedness``, ``rem:graph:settlement-party-mismatch`` and ``thm:graph:epoch-branch-fold`` (closure) govern these distinctions. The designed schedule is (`alg:actepoch:title-local`), with agreement across causal layers and co-epochal ties proved by (`prop:actepoch:title-local-equivalence`) for checked complete inputs; implementation remains held by (`open:actepoch:title-admission`). An unselected Ratify remains ordinary causal evidence, not a spent-state anchor or a veto.

**Algorithm (Local title completion)** · `alg:actepoch:title-local` Designed and unbuilt; graph/title owns this factorization and schedule under (`rule:actepoch:title-transition`). The paper basis is network-v0.37.4; the tagged statements cited here are closure statements. The supplied premises are internally checked, not certified historical facts.

Let P be the internally checked entering facts for the complete required domain, and U the finite set of current accepted acts with their public terms and asserted references. Current means completed in the current accepted group, not merely written in this epoch; the check includes the required references regardless of writing epoch. Earlier/current membership is a semantic distinction, not a stored epoch number. P includes prior completion, selected Ratify, prior Accept anchor/reference dispositions, required accepted/countable support, genesis and the shared state graph. It does not establish its own historical truth. Sources: (`def:actepoch:title-entering`), (`rule:actepoch:title-prior-facts`), (`def:actepoch:title-domain`) and (`prop:actepoch:title-sufficiency`).

Use counted precedence from network-v0.37.4 ``def:graph:counted-parent-relation``: an explicit asserted parent must complete strictly before its child, either earlier or through the current causal layering. An excluded citation contributes no counted edge. Ordinary accepted-act precedence is not a claim that a settlement involving that act succeeded. The reference graph contains act identities, not the two projections of an act as mutually ordering vertices; ``def:graph:dependent-act-set`` distinguishes those relations. All current asserted references relevant to the check are considered together, including settlement references irrespective of writing epoch, under ``itm:graph:settlement-structure-well-formedness``.

An eligible Accept has accepted Bid/Offer/Item support, the correct binding and target, its Bid reference counted before it, which for a prior Accept is part of its frozen counted-reference disposition, and exactly one counted spent-state anchor on that Item: completed genesis or the selected Ratify of a completed transfer. The seller is its author and the cited state's terminal Actor; its target is the Bid author. A transfer's terminal Actor is its buyer, so no search for a global owner is involved. Its anchor precedes this Accept. Other asserted parents are ordinary causal evidence, not additional title states merely because they occur on a path in the reference graph. Missing, plural or unfinished anchors and excluded forward citations give no eligible Accept. Sources: network-v0.37.4 ``def:graph:settlement-parties``, ``itm:graph:settlement-structure-authorization``, ``itm:graph:settlement-structure-well-formedness``; (`def:actepoch:title-state`), (`def:actepoch:title-transfer`) and (`rule:actepoch:title-transition`).

For a prior Accept, eligibility is its checked entering disposition, together with the required coherent public terms; it is not recomputed using newly completed states. For a current Accept it is calculated from P and the complete current group. A prior unsupported Accept stays unsupported. An earlier Ratify whose Accept citation was excluded is not reconsidered when that Accept appears. Missing required support for checking a claimed prior disposition is different from a supplied coherent disposition saying that its referent was absent. Sources: (`rule:actepoch:title-prior-facts`), (`ex:actepoch:title-forward`) and the pending-handshake row of (`def:actepoch:title-entering`).

For each as-yet-uncompleted Accept a, define Q(a) as the current Ratifys r satisfying all Ratify-local clauses: accepted membership, exact Accept and Bid binding, required family and public terms, the Ratify's Bid and Accept references counted before it, Ratify author equal to Bid author, target equal to Accept author, and its counted-reference cycle-membership check. These clauses can be evaluated without selecting a completion for a: their seller is the recorded Accept author; equality of that author to the cited state's terminal Actor belongs to Accept eligibility. If a is eligible, its correct current Ratifys are exactly Q(a); otherwise none completes it. This factorization expresses network-v0.37.4 ``itm:graph:settlement-structure-completeness``, ``itm:graph:settlement-structure-reference-binding``, ``itm:graph:settlement-structure-authorization``, ``itm:graph:settlement-structure-well-formedness`` and ``rem:graph:settlement-clause-structure``, not a weaker recognition predicate.

The selected completion C(a) is its supplied prior selected Ratify when P says a is completed; otherwise C(a) is min-by-byte-identifier Q(a) exactly when a is eligible and Q(a) is nonempty; otherwise it is absent. A prior Ratify is never a fresh current candidate. Each newly completed a adds exactly one child of its eligible anchor state, with a as the child identity, C(a) as completing evidence and its Bid author as terminal Actor. A distinct eligible Accept may add another child to the same state, including an interior state. The completed state associated with a Ratify r exists precisely when r is C(a) for its exact Accept a; an unselected repetition supplies no such anchor. Sources: network-v0.37.4 ``def:graph:settlement-structure``, ``rem:graph:title-order-irrelevance``, ``thm:graph:epoch-branch-fold``; (`rule:actepoch:title-completion`).

A cycle is a nonempty directed closed chain of co-epochal asserted-parent dependencies subjected to the counted-parent check, including self-citation, not a cycle inferred from branch competition or common endpoints. Its members fail authorization. A required authorization whose only support is those members fails as well; a cycle is not repaired by deleting one edge according to traversal order. The strict counted relation used for a successful handshake is acyclic. Detecting a cycle among accepted acts is represented structural failure, not permission to reject all accepted acts or to erase unrelated valid handshakes. The controlling statements are network-v0.37.4 ``def:graph:counted-parent-relation``, ``itm:graph:settlement-structure-well-formedness`` and ``rem:graph:title-order-irrelevance``; (`rule:actepoch:title-transition`).

Withdraw and Rescind do not enter Q or Accept eligibility as vetoes. Retain their identities and terms; attach each to every matching completed Accept by Bid and, for Rescind, original seller, retaining the cancellation for future matches. Each pair (Accept, cancellation) occurs once. A repeated Ratify adds no child or recipient event and retains its own cell at self; a failed handshake contributes no outward completion event. Existing persistent events remain. The standing owner applies its separate positivity and Registration predicates and accounts once per distinct act, never once per branch. Sources: network-v0.37.4 ``def:graph:branch-challenge``, ``thm:graph:epoch-branch-fold``; (`def:actepoch:title-challenges`), (`def:actepoch:title-outgoing`) and (`rem:actepoch:title-standing`).

This specification selects the least correct Ratify across the whole epoch regardless of its causal layer. A descendant citing a larger repetition has no completed-state anchor from it. A descendant citing the selected one can complete only if that exact Ratify is counted before it and its other clauses hold. The words “first correctly authored Ratify in the layer” in network-v0.37.4 ``alg:graph:branch-certificate-computation`` are read with the explicit least-epoch/least-identifier selection in ``def:graph:settlement-structure``, not as a second layer-priority tiebreak. The latter definition and ``thm:graph:settlement-order-freeness`` decide the meaning.

An anchor check may depend negatively on another completion as well as positively: after all potential anchor decisions are final, it must reject a plural qualifying anchor set, and it must not count a reference to an unselected Ratify as an anchor; that reference remains ordinary causal evidence. In particular, an Accept citing both C(b) and a repetition of b has exactly one anchor from those two citations, C(b), provided it is counted before the Accept; the repetition neither supplies a second anchor nor vetoes the first. These negative tests are stratified by the same strictly earlier Accept dependencies; the schedule does not run a monotone fixed point that adds any currently justified edge. Nor does it use a graph of only selected Ratifys before those Ratifys have been selected.

Two different graphs must not be confused. The accepted-reference check considers every relevant asserted incidence, including non-settlement parents, and identifies genuine co-epochal cycles without using the completion choices. The evaluator dependency graph has one decision vertex per current Accept and an edge from each possibly usable earlier anchor handshake. A failed counted incidence supplies no successful dependency. A cycle in the former makes its members fail authorization, and authorization depending only on those members fails as well; a cycle in the latter among successful candidates would contradict the strict counted paths witnessing its edges. Sharing a Bid, a seller, an Item or an already spent state creates neither kind of cycle. Sources: network-v0.37.4 ``def:graph:settlement-act-reference``, ``def:graph:counted-parent-relation``, ``itm:graph:settlement-structure-well-formedness``; (`rule:actepoch:title-transition`).

The cycle implementation can use strongly connected components on the current reference graph subject to the counted-placement check: mark a nontrivial component or a singleton self-loop as cyclic, retain that failure evidence, and resolve the acyclic remainder. Do not manufacture a counted edge for an excluded reference, and do not infer that every causally later handshake fails merely because an earlier accepted handshake failed. Failure propagates through the required authorization predicates: a necessary anchor supplied only by a cyclic or unfinished handshake cannot authorize a child; a valid independent genesis-rooted handshake still can. The counted-parent adapter must expose edge-level placement and cycle dispositions, not a host linear order advertised as counted precedence. These operands are the prior accepted/countable support of (`def:actepoch:title-entering`) and the complete current asserted-parent domain; no stored timestamp or new title fact is required.

First check the supplied domain and entering relations, index exact identities and public terms, and separate P from U. Resolve the complete asserted-reference domain and its placement dispositions. Detect cyclic components before treating any of their members as successful authorization support; do not turn an excluded or cyclic reference into an earlier parent by sorting input. A counted-parent result that contradicts these checks is invalid supplied evidence, whereas an accepted act whose own reference fails gets an unsupported disposition. Keep these outcomes distinct.

Next collect every current Ratify into its exact Accept's candidate bucket and evaluate all Ratify-local clauses Q before scheduling Accept eligibility. Include later causal layers. Keep existing completions from P frozen, and keep prior Accept dispositions frozen. Introduce valid current genesis roots with their counted placement; a root that does not precede a current Accept is not usable by that Accept. A prior eligible pending Accept can have a first current completion immediately after its complete Q bucket has been considered.

For each current Accept a, consider every asserted Ratify reference r that could supply a counted anchor on its Item. Resolve r's exact Accept b. If r can be a correct current completion candidate, it has counted b before r before a; therefore b is strictly earlier than a. Add a dependency from b's final completion decision to a's anchor check, even if r is not b's minimum and will turn out to be a repetition. For prior b, its selected completion is either already in P or is the first current completion fixed from b's frozen eligibility and complete Q bucket before current Accept scheduling; both are settled inputs to a. A reference failing required order, cycle, family or binding checks cannot supply an anchor and creates no successful authorization dependency. All potential usable anchors are inspected, including those needed to detect multiplicity; do not stop at the first one that succeeds.

Process the remaining current Accepts in any topological order of those dependencies, or, more conservatively, in the order that a topological ordering of the acyclic remainder of the complete current counted act graph, after cyclic components are marked, induces on its Accepts. For a, all possible earlier anchor handshakes are now final. Classify its asserted references using those final selections, calculate and store its anchor/reference disposition, and choose C(a) from its already complete Q bucket if eligible. Expose only this final C(a) and its new child; never expose the first Ratify encountered in a layer. Complete absence is also final and wakes dependent checks as a negative result. Current cycle-failed Accepts receive their failed disposition and no completion. A pending current Accept without a correct Ratify records its eligibility for a genuinely later completion.

After all these decisions, union all completed edges with the entering graph and calculate cancellation matches over the resulting graph. Preserve pending matches for later siblings. Emit the undated dispositions, selections and event additions; ordinary revision work remains its owner's operation and does not depend on a winning branch. This phase does not change any completion decision. No intermediate result escapes as a checked transition value if input validation or a declared resource allowance prevents completion.

**Proposition (Local title completion equivalence)** · `prop:actepoch:title-local-equivalence` For internally checked complete inputs in (`def:actepoch:title-domain`), (`alg:actepoch:title-local`) terminates, computes the declarative conjunction of network-v0.37.4 ``def:graph:settlement-structure`` and ``def:graph:settlement-parties`` (closure), and gives equal identity-indexed results for every admitted order. This is a conditional algorithm proof, not historical truth or executed evidence.

Proof of termination: there are finitely many accepted acts, reference incidences and candidate Ratifys. Cycle detection and placement classification are finite graph operations. Every unresolved successful anchor dependency b→a is witnessed by strict counted b→r→a, so a dependency cycle would imply a counted causal cycle, already excluded from successful authorization. Consequently the dependency graph is acyclic. Each current Accept is decided once after its predecessors, each candidate bucket is scanned finitely, and the final finite cancellation join terminates. A late alternative Ratify does not add a dependency from its ordinary causal ancestors' settlement successes to its own success: accepted precedence and title authorization are distinct predicates.

Proof of equivalence: begin with the internally checked prior facts and current genesis and, for each prior pending Accept b whose frozen disposition is eligible, C(b) = min Q(b), which reads no current Accept decision; when Q(b) is empty its completion is absent. These are the declarative constants for current Accept scheduling: a first current completion of prior b is derived from frozen eligibility and the complete candidate bucket, not assumed as an entering completion. Q is exact by the clause factorization: only the shared Accept eligibility remains to decide, and cancellation is no veto. Induct over the dependency DAG. Suppose all predecessors of a have their declarative completion. Every possible usable current anchor of a comes from such a predecessor, and every other relevant anchor is prior, genesis or such a first completion; no unexamined later Accept can supply a counted anchor. Thus the evaluator sees exactly the declarative completed-state anchors, including their multiplicity and terminal Actors, and computes exactly a's declarative eligibility. Its complete Q then yields exactly the declarative minimum or absence; a prior completion is never replaced. This proves the next induction step and, after finitely many steps, every selection and disposition. Child construction and cancellation matching are deterministic set constructions from those selections, so their results and event additions agree too.

Proof of schedule agreement: any admissible evaluator order settles a only after all its possible successful anchor predecessors, and uses the same fixed Q and P. For every dependency between current Accepts b→a, the acyclic remainder of the complete current counted act graph, after cyclic components are marked, contains the two direct edges b→r and r→a. Every topological order of that full graph therefore puts b before r before a; restricting the resulting ordered sequence to Accept vertices preserves b before a. Thus the induced order is a topological order of the dependency DAG and is covered by the same induction. Restricting the direct edge relation to Accept vertices before ordering is not this construction: it can erase the intervening Ratify and is not admitted. No new input premise is needed; the two edges are the counted incidences already required when a dependency is added. The same induction therefore applies to each order. Two incomparable Accepts cannot change one another's eligibility by consuming a state, taking a branch slot, cancelling completion or replacing prior evidence: none of those operations exists in the specification. Hence both orders yield equal identity-indexed facts, not merely isomorphic graphs. Permuting the presentation of U or the references changes neither the sets, counted relations nor identifier minimum, and therefore changes no result. This is an algorithm argument for the local undated scope; historical correctness still requires the separate seam check.

Let n be the number of distinct accepted act identities whose terms or dispositions this transition reads, including required prior support, not merely the number of new acts. Let e be their asserted-reference incidences, a the current Accept count, r the current Ratify count, t the completed-transfer count in the required outgoing graph, c the retained plus current cancellation count and g the required completed genesis count. Let b be the total bytes of the public terms and identifiers examined, and m the number of matching (Accept, cancellation) pairs emitted or checked, with m ≤ tc. These are input/work quantities, not chosen ceilings. The cumulative event census and ordinary-revision support have to be charged to their actual input sizes as well; a sparse set of newly touched Items does not bound their size (`def:actepoch:title-domain`).

A conservative implementation has the following polynomial upper bound, without relying on average-case hashing. Intern identity and term references by sorting, charging actual byte comparisons to b and their repeated comparison cost. Graph cycle checks and a topological order take O(n+e); a reachability table obtained by one search per vertex costs O(n(n+e)) time and O(n²+e) space if needed for the independent strict-precedence queries. Direct counted incidences still have to be present where the specification requires an explicit parent; transitive reachability is not a substitute for that reference. Complete candidate bucketing and minima cost O(n+e+r) indexed operations. The Accept dependency graph has at most e edges; all eligibility checks and its topological evaluation cost O(n+e+r). Entering graph closure, parties and selected-evidence checks cost O(n+e+t+g) after indexing, apart from the separate ordinary-revision checks.

A deliberately simple cancellation implementation tests every cancellation against every completed transfer, then inserts each match once: O(tc) predicate tests, O(m) output. Matching can later be indexed by Bid and seller, but that is an optimization, not a premise of this bound. Total structural work is therefore O(n(n+e)+tc+(n+e+t+c+g) log(n+e+t+c+g)), plus the measured byte-comparison/decoding work and the independently scoped ordinary-revision and standing computations. A coarse bit/byte-safe bound multiplies the indexed comparison terms by the maximum examined identifier/term length; no unbounded string comparison is counted as constant-time. Working storage is O(n²+e+t+c+g+m), plus supplied/owned bytes and event support. Omitting the optional reachability table reduces space when direct checked incidences and ranks suffice; the stated bound does not require that optimization.

The derivation depends on a decision being made once per Accept and on inspecting the full Ratify bucket once, not on enumerating candidate assignments in production. Exactly t+g shared state nodes suffice for t completed transfers over g present Items, by network-v0.37.4 ``thm:graph:epoch-branch-fold``; absent-genesis Items add no title root. Listing every root-to-state path or copying every ancestor into each child is outside this transition and can add quadratic work even for a chain. Retention can grow indefinitely over a run; neither the finite-input polynomial bound nor the node-count identity permits pruning an interior state, cancellation or event. Meter actual visited identities, incidences, candidate tests, attachment tests, byte work and output size; choose finite resource values only in the later measured implementation admission, not in this algorithm (`rule:actepoch:title-growth`).

**Proposition (Undated transition sufficiency)** · `prop:actepoch:title-sufficiency` Conditional on internally coherent entering facts with the dispositions above, the undated title transition for a complete current accepted group is determined without an earlier-epoch read or an epoch number. Proof: every structural predicate reads identity, accepted/countable membership, public fields, a completed parent state or a prior checked disposition. Earlier-versus-current membership distinguishes earlier completion; counted causal layers settle current precedence. Prior completion prevents a later replacement, while the specified minimum among eligible current Ratifys settles a current tie. Child construction depends on these predicates and not challenge dates. Challenge matching is identity/author matching, and retention preserves all future matches. The outgoing graph and matching facts are consequently determined by those operands. This proves sufficiency for the undated transition only: it does not infer historical correctness of the entering facts, dated challenges, a complete certificate or a winning branch. The proof's obligations are the concrete semantic projection and independent transition controls; no executed claim follows.

**Example (A later referent does not repair a forward citation)** · `ex:actepoch:title-forward` If an earlier Accept's anchor was not yet completed, supplying that anchor in this epoch does not make the Accept eligible: its earlier checked disposition remains unsupported. A fresh eligible Accept can cite the now-completed state. Likewise a Ratify that preceded its Accept is not reconsidered as a first completion merely because the Accept now exists.

**Example (Two children and a later challenge)** · `ex:actepoch:title-siblings` Two eligible Accepts spending one completed state, each with a completing Ratify, add two children. A later matching cancellation annotates both where its buyer/seller relation matches and propagates along their descendant paths; it removes neither child, changes neither identity nor previously selected completing Ratify, and changes no outward recipient under the explicit persistent-recipient rule.

**Rule (Growth and retention)** · `rule:actepoch:title-growth` Retain all completed states and edges and all relevant pending and attached evidence; an interior state cannot be dropped on the ground that it has been spent. network-v0.37.4 ``thm:graph:epoch-branch-fold`` (closure) states: “Therefore an Item with genesis has exactly one more state node than completed transfers, regardless of branching; only the fan-out of a state is unbounded.” Its proof also states: “This node bound does not bound replay or path-enumeration work, whose growth must be measured and disclosed separately from the one-cell-per-act allocation pass.” The graph is finite for finite input. Distinct cancellations and pending handshakes grow with distinct accepted acts; no constant cap, expiry or safe pruning rule is inferred. Shared prefixes avoid copying an ancestor for every descendant; they do not justify a bound on total state size or service work. Resource limits and partial-read policies must preserve a truthful unavailable result rather than silently omit branches.

**Definition (Outgoing title facts)** · `def:actepoch:title-outgoing` The transition computes the updated undated graph, new and retained handshake dispositions, selected completing evidence, cancellation facts and matches, and the applicable ordinary revision facts. It also supplies persistent settlement event inputs to the standing calculation. These outgoing values are calculated results under (`rule:actepoch:computed-publication`), not a second committed branch; a subsequent epoch supplies the necessary projection as its entering state. No global owner, winning edge, consumed-candidate set or stored completion epoch is an output of this concept.

**Rule (Title seam)** · `rule:actepoch:title-seam` For an already statically validated declared run, compare the next entering title facts with the semantic projection of previously computed outgoing facts for every Item and support subject the next calculation reads. Compare genesis, all relevant graph edges and their parent/evidence identities, checked reference dispositions, pending/completed handshakes, selected Ratify, cancellation identities and matches, and required revision facts; compare absence as well as presence. Derivable caches need not be independently compared when the representation excludes them. The run checker retains a calculated accumulated map across epochs that do not touch an Item, so that an untouched interval does not make an old Item look absent. An empty-origin run establishes initial absence; an unanchored interval leaves its initial register conditional. Persistent recipient events remain a cumulative comparison domain at every epoch, even when graph or challenge projections are sparse. The register is a checker result, not a new common document or an epoch-number member of state. This refines the title coordinate of (`rule:actepoch:parent-connection`) without rerunning formation or selecting an authoritative run.

**Definition (Dated title read)** · `def:actepoch:title-read` A title read names a declared run, an evaluation point in it and a genesis-rooted timeline. It derives act writing/completion locations from membership in that run, selects the graph prefix at that point and follows the named path. If the path has not completed there, it has no title there. It returns the terminal Actor and applicable challenge evidence without selecting another path on the caller's behalf. Reading earlier points from only the final undated state is insufficient: the run, or equivalent admitted evidence of its relevant partition, must be supplied. Missing run evidence leaves the dated read unavailable and does not weaken the local static verdict.

**Definition (Computed title certificate)** · `def:actepoch:title-summary` The certificate summarizes the graph for the named run prefix, its paths and terminal Actors, pending challenges and epoch-stamped edge challenges. In that computation, cancellation identities are associated with their writing epochs from the run, and attachments can be grouped as network-v0.37.4 ``def:graph:branch-challenge`` (closure) specifies. The certificate is neither entering state nor an additional output commitment. An optional published copy is a vendor-named courtesy under (`rule:actepoch:computed-publication`); it has no authority in acceptance and no conformance claimed by merely being served.
