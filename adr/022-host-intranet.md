Depends on: (`rec:storage:decision`), (`rec:observe:decision`), (`rec:devnet:decision`). ## Host intranet · `sec:intranet:record`

**Definition (Host intranet)** · `def:intranet:host-services` The host intranet consists of a peer host's links to services operated for that instance alone: its PostgreSQL store (`rec:storage:decision`), its Elements node's RPC through pn-wallet (`rule:devnet:custody`), and its telemetry collector (`amend:observe:export-credentials`). These links are distinct from the peer network: no peer reaches these service endpoints, and nothing on them forms part of the network's public protocol. The service data carried across them is public; connection and authentication material is instance-private.

**Rule (Private reachability)** · `rule:intranet:secret-reachability` Endpoint, socket path, port, user, password, proxy address and certificate material are private keys of the instance, like its signing keys. They remain secret-owned from acquisition through retained state, authentication and error paths under (`dec:observe:secrets`) and (`inv:observe:secret-boundary`). A public case declaration does not declassify reachability. Public service requests, results and diagnostics remain fully observable under (`rule:observe:privacy`); observation of private material carries only its role and secret marker. Neither transport case permits ordinary private copies or printable dependency errors containing private bytes.

**Case (Implementation-guarded intranet)** · `case:intranet:guarded` The implementation authenticates each service and protects the confidentiality and integrity of each session itself over the intervening path, typically using TLS with server authentication from secret-owned trust material. This case is required when the operator cannot provide a secure path, such as a database reached over a network outside the operator's control. Authentication protects the intended service and session after login, not merely the exchange of credentials. Admission is held under (`open:intranet:guarded-transport`).

**Case (Host-secured intranet)** · `case:intranet:host-secured` The implementation relies on a secure intranet supplied by the host operator and connects to the supplied endpoint without transport security of its own. The path may be a same-machine Unix-domain socket or loopback connection, a secure tunnel such as WireGuard or SSH reached at its local end, or a secure backbone under the operator's control. Confidentiality, integrity and authenticity of the service at the far end are the operator's duty. The implementation makes no claim to establish those properties for this path; neither an address nor successful login proves them. The operator must protect local endpoint ownership and access, tunnel endpoints and the full backbone as applicable. Endpoint and credentials remain private under (`rule:intranet:secret-reachability`); service login does not replace the operator's transport duty.

**Decision (First realization)** · `dec:intranet:host-secured-first` The first realization implements (`case:intranet:host-secured`) and holds (`case:intranet:guarded`). Same-machine service placement in the compute and development-network deployments satisfies the intended deployment shape without a TLS component inside the implementation. A host-provided secure tunnel or backbone extends this case to remote services without changing the implementation's transport-security role. This removes TLS admission from the first storage route, while preserving the secret-terminating shim and every private-state and authentication obligation. It is a deployment assumption with an explicit non-claim, not a weakening of secret typing or a claim that every private network is secure.

**Invariant (Explicit transport case)** · `inv:intranet:explicit-case` Each instance explicitly declares its host-intranet case as a public bootstrap setting: host-secured or guarded. Naming the case reveals no reachability. Acquisition refuses a missing, unknown or held guarded case before a service connection is attempted, logging a bounded cause that carries no private value. The implementation never infers the case from an address, socket, port or available credentials and never falls back silently. The declaration is separate from secret connection material and is available before any database read; it is not a protocol rule or an alternative store of binding policy. Acceptance of host-secured records the operator's duty, not verification that the path satisfies it.

**Open Question (Guarded transport admission)** · `open:intranet:guarded-transport` Owner: the storage, wallet and telemetry integration implementers for their respective links, with independent integration review. Trigger: a deployment whose operator cannot provide a secure path. Admission requires service authentication and full-session protection without ordinary retained private reachability, trust material or private error payloads, with adversarial execution evidence under (`inv:observe:secret-boundary`). The TLS source evaluation (`amend:storage:tls-evaluation`) and its independent review are inputs, not admission of a stock component. Until a conforming transport is admitted for each affected integration and this case is enabled explicitly, (`inv:intranet:explicit-case`) refuses it. Remote location alone does not trigger the hold when the operator supplies the secure path of (`case:intranet:host-secured`).

**Amendment (Guarded transport inputs)** · `amend:intranet:guarded-inputs` (`open:intranet:guarded-transport`) has two inputs. For services that speak TLS, including guarded PostgreSQL, the route is unmodified rustls 0.23.46 with tokio-rustls 0.26.6: a constant public placeholder server name, SNI and resumption disabled, no client authentication, logging disabled, a shim-owned verifier borrowing secret-owned trust material and returning only unit certificate errors, and secret-safe Debug for the verifier and wrapped socket. SNI-dependent endpoints fail closed and client-certificate configuration refuses at acquisition; the retained peer chain and verifier temporary-state residue still require an explicit secrecy disposition and witnesses before admission. This refines the adaptation-target framing of (`amend:storage:tls-evaluation`) without enabling TLS. For a transport the implementation defines itself, the input is key agreement through (`dec:crypto:base-library`) with separately reviewed RustCrypto AEAD and HKDF; service authentication, transcript binding and full-session protection must be specified and witnessed, not inferred from ECDH or ElligatorSwift. Both routes remain held; neither changes (`case:intranet:host-secured`).

**Preface (Signed authentication token preference)** · `pref:intranet:signed-tokens` Where the implementation chooses an authentication token and the receiver can verify one, prefer a signed JWT. This governs host-intranet service authentication under (`rule:intranet:secret-reachability`) in both (`case:intranet:host-secured`) and (`case:intranet:guarded`); it does not govern acts submitted through the public API, which retain their own signatures under (`rule:api:submission`). A JWT carries issuer, audience, expiry and scope; a receiver verifies it with a public key without keeping a shared signing secret, and exposure is bounded by its validity and authorized scope. A static opaque shared bearer string has no such self-contained limits and normally lasts until rotation. Prefer ES256K, the registered JOSE algorithm for ECDSA over secp256k1 with SHA-256, produced through (`dec:crypto:base-library`); JOSE fixes ECDSA here rather than the default Schnorr choice. Pin the algorithm in the verifier's trusted policy, refuse none and shared-secret HMAC algorithms, and never select an algorithm from an untrusted token. Another asymmetric algorithm requires a per-link exception with evidence that the receiver cannot verify ES256K, and any different library also satisfies (`rule:crypto:asymmetric-exceptions`). A protocol-fixed login method has its own per-link exception naming that method and its reason. A valid token remains a bearer credential: signing neither encrypts it nor prevents replay within its authorized validity.

**Rule (Token verification and custody)** · `rule:intranet:token-verification` Under (`pref:intranet:signed-tokens`), verify the signature with a trusted issuer key, the pinned algorithm and key type, issuer, audience, required expiry and not-before, and scope for the requested operation. The initial verification profile permits at most 30 seconds of clock skew on the validity checks; each link declares its token lifetime and may choose a smaller skew, never an implicit or unbounded tolerance. NumericDate values are seconds since 1970-01-01T00:00:00Z; logs use explicit UTC. Missing or malformed fields, an unknown key, algorithm mismatch, failed signature, wrong issuer or audience, invalid time interval or insufficient scope refuse with a bounded cause containing neither token nor claims. The instance signing key and the bearer token, including decoded claim values and intermediate encoding buffers, stay secret-owned under (`dec:observe:secrets`) and (`inv:observe:secret-boundary`); the public key and claim schema are public. No shared-secret algorithm or ordinary credential-bearing parser error is admitted. Concrete signed-object framing, issuer authority, key rotation, validation and migration evidence remain subject to (`gate:identity:implementation`) and (`open:crypto:dependency-admission`) before a token integration is enabled.
