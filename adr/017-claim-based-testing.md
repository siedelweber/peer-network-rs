## Claim-based testing · `sec:testing:record`

**Rule (Public-record conformance)** · `rule:testing:public-record-conformance` A repository conformance claim covers only a predicate a third party can decide deterministically from the complete public record: the canonical package, its disclosed effective history and referenced public evidence, their declared theory and procedure editions, and the exact published inputs of a scoped witness. Two honest checkers using those same inputs and the declared exact interpretation must obtain the same predicate result; hardware, scheduling, elapsed time, sampling or acquisition truth, continuous availability, private search effort and an unanchored timestamp's truth are not inputs that the record establishes. A published assertion is checkable for presence and consistency, not thereby for truth. Each executed claimed part names its deciding predicate, required public inputs and witness route by label. A scope-only classification locates the predicate by its paper label and names the witness template; it must name its precise predicate and public inputs before advancing beyond scope-only. Missing evidence refuses the route; a proposed route is not an executed witness. This narrows the repository's claims without changing the paper or judging its requirements. For a proposition or explanatory remark, claimed identifies a decidable predicate within the scope; it does not turn the paper statement into a deployment duty. A mixed condition is split rather than either claiming its empirical premise or discarding its checkable publication duty. A split may instead distinguish witness routes: the included route realizes the tagged duty, while an excluded alternative remains conditional without excluding any part of that duty. The classification below fixes the conformance scope at network-v0.37.1; execution status remains governed by (`rule:testing:results`).

**Record (Deployment replay witness route)** · `rec:testing:deployment-replay-route` For a classified included part, recover its complete canonical inputs and historical declarations, validate the declared grammar and bounds, and recompute the named predicate using exact arithmetic or the independently checked certificate. Compare complete values, not a producer's success flag or an unbound digest. Negative controls remove an input, change the selected edition or alter the asserted result; they must refuse at that predicate. Publication and restoration cases supply only their registered finite scope. Terminal feed, CAN, empirical acquisition and live host workflows acquire no implementation status through this route. A row with scope-only status names a possible deterministic route, not a claim that all inputs, consumers or executions already exist. The designed binary64 route (`req:design:binary64-admission`) claims no execution here: its eventual inclusion and exclusion predicates are checked against the fixed original question and actual published enclosure, while implementation of canonical verification and completion availability remain held under (`resol:design:replay-paper-contract`); the released principle supplies design scope, not execution evidence.

**Convention (Conditional deployment dependencies)** · `conv:testing:deployment-dependencies` A conformance dependency is an author's explicit decision about which part supplies a premise. A labelled paragraph, obligation, input row or test-site claim mentioning an excluded obligation or a split statement records either a relies citation naming the excluded clause and what rests on it, or a realizes citation naming the claimed part it uses or realizes. Repeated mentions of one target in a paragraph share that paragraph's explicit decision; classification identities are not self-dependencies. Realizing a conditional repository record means using its statement without its marked premise; the citation names that bounded use rather than asserting the premise. Ordinary citations do not propagate conditionality. Only relies edges propagate an excluded premise through another conditional statement, to a fixed point including cycles. The premise form remains attention without a design or evidence route and does not itself create conformance reliance. A scoped check of supplied bytes does not inherit their acquisition truth merely by mentioning the relevant deployment condition. The checked generated edge dataset retains every explicit decision and its reason; missing or stale edges and unmarked relevant mentions refuse with their source, target and regeneration command. This separates external premises from checkable publication duties instead of making a connected citation graph uniformly conditional.

**Convention (Deployment classification schema)** · `conv:testing:deployment-schema` The version-one namespace org.peer-network.testing.deployment-claims carries authored statement and clause rows only. The strict schema in ci/deployment_claims.py requires single-line strings: kind, name, paper_tag, classification, included, excluded, reason, witness_route and evidence_status; a clause adds parent. Repository classifications are claimed, split, not-claimed and non-obligation. The inventory’s material_class separately records the paper’s exact-label closure, oracle, courtesy or mathematics class; statement rows join by name and clause rows by parent. Neither axis determines the other. The last class denotes a heading, clarification, proof case or unchosen design option that imposes no obligation, not an excluded duty. Every paper inventory identity occurs once and every clause parent resolves. Scope-only rows locate their predicate by the exact paper label and their route by the named template; the included and excluded parts are explicit. Before a row gains execution status, its deciding predicate and complete public inputs must be named individually in the realizing evidence. The generated org.peer-network.testing.deployment-edges dataset carries source, target, relation, reason and exact conditional_on closure. Its generated TOML intermediary is not authored evidence. After rendering changed citation templates, run python3 -B ci/deployment_claims.py generate, then documents generate write and documents render write under (`dec:output:policy`); checking compares the complete projection, including stale rows after removed citations.

**Decision (Claims as evidence)** · `dec:testing:evidence` Testing is claim-based: a claim states what a test establishes, with the premises and scope that make that statement checkable. Its authored label is minted beside the test that first establishes it, so requirements, decisions, contracts and record gates can cite the evidence rather than a function name or a passing suite total. A test inventory label identifies an executable asset; it does not state its proposition or establish correctness (`sig:labels:profiles`), (`cav:labels:assets`). The discipline begins before domain implementation, together with (`rec:observe:decision`); a trace of work and a test of its claim serve distinct purposes.

**Rule (Test documentation)** · `rule:testing:site` A covered test's documentation comment contains, in fixed order, a prose gloss, an authored label of kind claim on its own line, and the derived label of kind test as the last line. The gloss is a real sentence stating what the test establishes and why it matters, with relevant premises; respelling a function identifier with spaces is not a gloss. The first test establishing a statement carries the bare claim mint. A sibling test establishing that same statement carries a parenthesised citation to that mint, followed by its own derived test label; shared coverage has one mint and several citations. Distinct propositions need distinct claims, and broader wording cannot erase a test's restricted premises. Claim names are authored, never derived from function identifiers. The test label follows its profile's derivation and placement under (`sig:labels:profiles`).

**Convention (Owner registers)** · `conv:testing:registers` Every package owner that mints claims keeps a claim-area register in its own prose, one entry per minted area. Each entry's head prose states the stake: what is lost if that area's claims fail. The gate compares the area census with the register, rejects missing or stale entries and requires review of the stated stakes; mere label resolution cannot judge whether prose conveys the risk. Registers describe evidence ownership, not additional claim mints. Cross-owner document citations use the declared imports and ordinary resolution rules of (`rec:labels:decision`).

**Rule (Evidence links and holds)** · `rule:testing:coverage` Concept requirements, design decisions and verification plans, and records' gates cite the test-site claims that evidence them. A checked evidence relation records each obligation, its realizing decisions or plans, the supporting owner-qualified claims, their test sites and the relevant premises. Every requirement is evidenced by claims and every claim by a test is the query in both directions: no requirement disappears from the obligation census, and every minted claim resolves to at least one covered test, including every sibling citation. Internal claims may support a local contract or gate without claiming a concept requirement. An obligation or plan without a claim remains in an explicit labelled hold naming owner, missing evidence and closing bite; a future name in a plan is no mint, resolving citation or executed evidence. A hold is disclosed incompleteness, not a passing obligation. The closing bite may retain only holds outside its claimed supported surface (`gate:design:implementation-evidence`).

**Rule (Execution verdicts)** · `rule:testing:results` A claim cited or minted by a failing test is a failing claim, even when another supporting test passes. Join all supporting test identities to actual runner results for the checked source and declared target, features and profile; a missing, ignored, filtered, stale or unexecuted result is held, never passed. Failure takes precedence over holds, and passing requires all required supporting executions to pass with their premises satisfied. Expected rejection inside a negative test is not test failure when the harness confirms its asserted rejection. An obligation may declare combined_claim naming its already minted combined claim; every other owner-qualified label in that row's claims list is a required member. Membership never mints and ordinary comments never create evidence edges. The combined claim conjoins its own sites with each member's complete evidence, recursively; failure dominates holds. Missing members, absent mints, duplicate declarations and cycles refuse the join. The owning obligation also checks all these claims directly. Propagate failed and held claims to dependent obligations. Preserve oracle provenance, exact inputs, expected predicates, execution configuration and unresolved limits; test counts and broad suite success cannot replace that evidence. A finite fixture claim does not prove an unrestricted theorem. For the implemented package-configuration join of (`amend:harness:registry-join`), the required evidence is the declared set of supporting site/configuration pairs, not the union of test names across feature selections. One minted proposition spanning configurations passes only when every required pair has its own matching passed result; a failed pair fails it and a missing pair holds it. Configuration applicability is explicit and reviewed with the proposition, so a cfg-excluded site cannot silently remove a half that the claim asserts. The existing single-configuration results keep their scope; extending the join supplies new evidence rather than retroactively widening those results.

**Rule (Paper correspondence)** · `rule:testing:paper` Paper statements remain exhibited under (`setup:design:authority`) until the existing import path supplies a checked owner and release correspondence. Tests mint this repository's claims for the cases or properties they actually establish and relate them to the exhibited statement; they never mint the paper's labels. Once an import is established, use ordinary imported citations under (`rec:labels:decision`) without changing the claim's scope. Independent oracles and explicit numerical or deployment holds remain required; matching a production calculation to itself is not independent evidence.

**Rule (Claim evolution)** · `rule:testing:evolution` Under (`rule:compatibility:pre-one`), claim labels may be withdrawn or renamed freely before 1.0, with citing tests, documents and evidence relations moved in the same change. Removing the minting test while retaining sibling evidence transfers the single mint to a remaining establishing test. A changed proposition is reviewed with its tests and dependent obligations; an old name cannot conceal weaker evidence. No retained alias, migration or deprecation path is owed before 1.0.
