## Claim-based testing · `sec:testing:record`

**Decision (Claims as evidence)** · `dec:testing:evidence` Testing is claim-based: a claim states what a test establishes, with the premises and scope that make that statement checkable. Its authored label is minted beside the test that first establishes it, so requirements, decisions, contracts and record gates can cite the evidence rather than a function name or a passing suite total. A test inventory label identifies an executable asset; it does not state its proposition or establish correctness (`sig:labels:profiles`), (`cav:labels:assets`). The discipline begins before domain implementation, together with (`rec:observe:decision`); a trace of work and a test of its claim serve distinct purposes.

**Rule (Test documentation)** · `rule:testing:site` A covered test's documentation comment contains, in fixed order, a prose gloss, an authored label of kind claim on its own line, and the derived label of kind test as the last line. The gloss is a real sentence stating what the test establishes and why it matters, with relevant premises; respelling a function identifier with spaces is not a gloss. The first test establishing a statement carries the bare claim mint. A sibling test establishing that same statement carries a parenthesised citation to that mint, followed by its own derived test label; shared coverage has one mint and several citations. Distinct propositions need distinct claims, and broader wording cannot erase a test's restricted premises. Claim names are authored, never derived from function identifiers. The test label follows its profile's derivation and placement under (`sig:labels:profiles`).

**Convention (Owner registers)** · `conv:testing:registers` Every package owner that mints claims keeps a claim-area register in its own prose, one entry per minted area. Each entry's head prose states the stake: what is lost if that area's claims fail. The gate compares the area census with the register, rejects missing or stale entries and requires review of the stated stakes; mere label resolution cannot judge whether prose conveys the risk. Registers describe evidence ownership, not additional claim mints. Cross-owner document citations use the declared imports and ordinary resolution rules of (`rec:labels:decision`).

**Rule (Evidence links and holds)** · `rule:testing:coverage` Concept requirements, design decisions and verification plans, and records' gates cite the test-site claims that evidence them. A checked evidence relation records each obligation, its realizing decisions or plans, the supporting owner-qualified claims, their test sites and the relevant premises. Every requirement is evidenced by claims and every claim by a test is the query in both directions: no requirement disappears from the obligation census, and every minted claim resolves to at least one covered test, including every sibling citation. Internal claims may support a local contract or gate without claiming a concept requirement. An obligation or plan without a claim remains in an explicit labelled hold naming owner, missing evidence and closing bite; a future name in a plan is no mint, resolving citation or executed evidence. A hold is disclosed incompleteness, not a passing obligation. The closing bite may retain only holds outside its claimed supported surface (`gate:design:implementation-evidence`).

**Rule (Execution verdicts)** · `rule:testing:results` A claim cited or minted by a failing test is a failing claim, even when another supporting test passes. Join all supporting test identities to actual runner results for the checked source and declared target, features and profile; a missing, ignored, filtered, stale or unexecuted result is held, never passed. Failure takes precedence over holds, and passing requires all required supporting executions to pass with their premises satisfied. Expected rejection inside a negative test is not test failure when the harness confirms its asserted rejection. Propagate failed and held claims to dependent obligations. Preserve oracle provenance, exact inputs, expected predicates, execution configuration and unresolved limits; test counts and broad suite success cannot replace that evidence. A finite fixture claim does not prove an unrestricted theorem.

**Rule (Paper correspondence)** · `rule:testing:paper` Paper statements remain exhibited under (`setup:design:authority`) until the existing import path supplies a checked owner and release correspondence. Tests mint this repository's claims for the cases or properties they actually establish and relate them to the exhibited statement; they never mint the paper's labels. Once an import is established, use ordinary imported citations under (`rec:labels:decision`) without changing the claim's scope. Independent oracles and explicit numerical or deployment holds remain required; matching a production calculation to itself is not independent evidence.

**Rule (Claim evolution)** · `rule:testing:evolution` Under (`rule:compatibility:pre-one`), claim labels may be withdrawn or renamed freely before 1.0, with citing tests, documents and evidence relations moved in the same change. Removing the minting test while retaining sibling evidence transfers the single mint to a remaining establishing test. A changed proposition is reviewed with its tests and dependent obligations; an old name cannot conceal weaker evidence. No retained alias, migration or deprecation path is owed before 1.0.

**Convention (Profile owners)** · `conv:testing:profiles` The adopted owners DOCUMENTS, INTERCHANGE, CLICOMMON, EXECWRAP already activate profile.tests-conform and profile.claims-conform in .linter/policies.toml (`sig:labels:profiles`). The claim policy checks the placement and authored claim at a covered test and depends on the test policy: a claim answers to the test it documents. Retain both for these owners. Each new first-party package owner activates both when introduced, with its area register and import declarations in that bite; this is an accepted activation obligation, not a policy change in this record. ROOT's prose does not acquire a test profile merely by citing claims, and no vendor activation is implied.

**Proposal (Evidence coverage gate)** · `proposal:testing:gate` Accepted without additional condition. The foundation implementer adds a gate beside the deployed linter: obtain the covered-test and claim/citation census from checked source, join it to the requirement, decision, plan and record-gate obligation register, owner-area registers and runner results, then evaluate (`rule:testing:coverage`) and (`rule:testing:results`). Enumerate supported build configurations and account for generated tests through checked expansion or generator mappings; an uninspectable or omitted site fails coverage. Reject unresolved or duplicate mints, orphan claims, missing test results, uncited evidence presented as coverage, missing requirement edges and holds without owners or closing conditions. Compare the obligation register to the source label census so omitting a row cannot make coverage green. The design's proposed names are excluded from the minted-claim census. Add probes for shared claims with one failed sibling, stale results, missing areas, removed obligations and planned names mistaken for evidence. Detect simple function-name respellings mechanically; require item-specific reviewer confirmation that each gloss is a sentence expressing the actual assertion and its stake. The linter proves placement and resolution, not prose meaning or theorem truth. No test suppression waives this evidence contract (`dec:lint:policy`). The mechanism and new-owner activations land as code and declarations in the closing bites, not in this documentation change.

New checks mint their own test-site claims and this gate cites them when they exist. Owner: the foundation implementer and repository reviewer. This gate is held until those changes and executions land; a green documentation gate does not close it.
