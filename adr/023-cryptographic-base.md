## Cryptographic base · `sec:crypto:record`

**Decision (Base library)** · `dec:crypto:base-library` Prefer Blockstream Research's secp256k1-zkp, the libsecp256k1 fork used by Elements, through its Rust bindings for the implementation's asymmetric cryptography. Use BIP-340 Schnorr where the implementation chooses a signature scheme and ECDSA where a protocol fixes it. One curve and one implementation lineage let act authentication, wallet operations and the Elements evidence in (`rec:devnet:decision`) and (`rec:storage:decision`) share reviewed machinery. The constant-time C lineage, extensive tests and published audits provide review depth; Elements-native commitment and proof modules avoid a second cryptographic library. These are reasons for a base, not a claim that every module or version shares one audit or that Rust bindings use a single C archive. The paper at network-v0.32.0 leaves the realization, key management and algorithm migration to deployment in ``rem:graph:authentication-realization-out-of-scope`` while requiring ``post:graph:act-authentication-requirements``. This record chooses implementation machinery, not a new paper premise.

**Rule (Operation scope)** · `rule:crypto:operation-scope` Use (`dec:crypto:base-library`) for signatures, curve key derivation and tweaking, and, when required and exposed by the admitted binding, multi-party and adaptor signatures, Pedersen commitments, range proofs and surjection proofs. For a transport the implementation defines itself, use its ECDH and ElligatorSwift key-agreement facilities; the symmetric half uses separately reviewed RustCrypto AEAD and HKDF primitives, as in the BIP-324 construction. ECDH alone does not authenticate a service, and this library choice admits no transport protocol. Services that require TLS retain a TLS integration under (`case:intranet:guarded`). Every standalone hash, MAC and KDF uses reviewed RustCrypto crates, including SHA-256 digests, HMAC, PBKDF2 and HKDF. Internal hashing within a base-library operation, including ECDH, BIP-340 and ElligatorSwift, belongs to that operation and is not a standalone primitive selection. The optional hashes feature of the base binding is not selected. Those primitives are within this division of work, not exceptions to the asymmetric base.

**Rule (Asymmetric exceptions)** · `rule:crypto:asymmetric-exceptions` Another asymmetric library requires a recorded exception naming the operation the base library cannot perform, the consumer or fixed protocol that requires it, the evidence of that limitation and the replacement's review. Missing bindings are an admission question, not permission to claim a capability or silently add another implementation. A protocol-fixed algorithm still uses the base where it supports that algorithm. Each exception preserves (`inv:observe:secret-boundary`) and records its feature and dependency cost.

**Rule (Secret-owning integration)** · `rule:crypto:secret-integration` Cryptographic keys, nonce material, blinding factors and shared secrets remain secret-owned under (`dec:observe:secrets`) and (`inv:observe:secret-boundary`). **Amendment (Secret home)** · `amend:crypto:secret-home` pn-secret-home owns each secret in one fixed anonymous mapping on Linux x86_64. Process-start establishment clears dumpability and sets both core limits to zero, then reads them back, and requires an explicit page-multiple total mapping budget within the current kernel memlock limit. The page size is read once from the Linux auxiliary vector before clearing dumpability and cached as public platform metadata, since later auxiliary-vector opens may be denied. A process-wide reservation counter bounds mappings under the one declared budget; budget changes refuse. Each allocation is page-rounded, private, readable and writable, advised no-dump and no-fork, and locked before secret material enters it. Advice or locking failure refuses. OS entropy fills the mapping directly and a partial entropy error drops and erases it. Import borrows an explicitly protected staging carrier, copies directly into the mapping, and erases staging on success and refusal; staging also erases on drop. Earlier caller copies and compiler move temporaries remain outside that guarantee. Home has no Copy, Clone, Display, serde, Deref or DerefMut and no public byte export. Constant Debug observes no bytes or addresses. Only the handle can move or enter a collection. The private slice lifetime is tied to an exclusive owner borrow; destruction erases the entire initialized mapping with zeroize before unlocking and unmapping it. An inherited handle in a fork child never dereferences or releases the absent mapping; inherited protection refuses acquisition by process identity. Identity combines the numeric PID with the device and inode of a retained open PID-namespace handle and a retained proc task-directory handle, not the PID alone. A fresh stat-file metadata lookup through that task handle must succeed without opening another descriptor; the handle never retargets to a later task reusing the PID, so an inherited owner cannot revive after the original task exits. The open handle keeps that namespace alive and prevents reuse of its identity while an owner remains: a fork within it has a different PID, while a fork into another PID namespace has a different namespace identity even when both PIDs are one. Acquisition and checked byte access establish that identity through safe procfs metadata and the fresh task-directory lookup before touching the mapping. Drop checks it exactly once, then erases through the sole private slice constructor without another fallible identity lookup; the test-only erase observer uses that same already-established ownership. This removes the check-between-checks panic from destruction. The six unsafe sites and their expressions remain unchanged; the slice site states the caller-established identity premise explicitly. The task lookup follows the retained descriptor, never a newly resolved numeric PID path. It uses stat rather than open, so EMFILE and ENFILE cannot prevent it by exhausting descriptor slots. An unreadable identity refuses acquisition/access and makes Drop leave the mapping and reservation untouched rather than risk dereferencing a child's absent mapping; erasure and release therefore require a readable genuine procfs namespace view in the owner. LSM or seccomp denial of metadata syscalls, loss or replacement of that procfs view, or invalidated held descriptors can still make ownership unprovable and leave a mapping unerased, locked and reserved. Touching it in that state would risk accessing a fork child's absent or replaced mapping; no unchecked erasure fallback is admitted. Process termination before Drop, including abort or fatal signals, likewise cannot execute user-space erasure. Unlock or unmap errors occur after erasure and can retain only the erased allocation, not its former secret. Home::complete is the explicit consuming checked route: it returns ownership refusal without byte access or both unconfirmed cleanup flags after erasure, caches that outcome against a fallback retry, and releases the reservation only after confirmed unmap. A failed live mapping stays reserved until process exit; ordinary Drop remains best effort and also decrements only on successful unmap. No asynchronous error reporting or guaranteed erasure under a compromised process environment is claimed. The supported environment must keep that view available and must not substitute it. The Linux namespaces and PID-namespace contracts supply this identity argument; the finite same-PID/distinct-kernel-namespace and dead-task-handle controls witness the guard, not an executed namespace fork or forced PID reuse. The alternatives were not adopted: an at-fork hook would need registration outside the six admitted sites, and a bare range-presence check could mistake a child's replacement mapping for the original. No additional unsafe site or numerical declaration is introduced. The supported consumer must not reset process protections, fork while borrowing the mapping or introduce concurrent process-policy changes. Kernel read-back and the finite refusal controls establish the exercised Linux behavior, not a guarantee against privileged debuggers, preexisting process readers, a compromised kernel, hibernation or secret copies inside cryptographic algorithms. The C library's internal temporaries and binding algorithm buffers remain outside owned-home erasure under (`amend:crypto:owned-secret-carriers`). No signing operation or wallet migration is enabled; (`open:crypto:dependency-admission`) stays open for operation evidence and independent review. The source exception and its inventory follow (`amend:lint:secret-home-unsafe`).
