## Development performance · `sec:devperf:record`

**Decision (Short feedback)** · `dec:devperf:feedback` Development work is designed to finish quickly: a slow test is an engineering defect to investigate, and a slow build is a build-configuration defect to correct. Small fixtures, functional substitutes and shared setup preserve useful evidence without repeatedly paying unrelated costs. Neither concern changes what a claim establishes (`rec:testing:decision`). A correct but slow execution remains correct; the performance obligation is visible as a warning and an owned finding, never disguised as a test failure or dismissed as acceptable latency.

**Rule (Timing is never a verdict)** · `rule:devperf:nonverdict` Every exceeded wall-time budget and timing regression emits a warning and a persistent performance finding with operation/test label, measured duration, budget, baseline when available, source/configuration, execution outcome and owner. It never fails a test, claim, benchmark, tier or gate, changes an exit code, or turns completed evidence into a hold. Do not retry, skip, filter or terminate a test because it took too long; retain its original result and measurement. Budget timers observe completion and are not timeout settings. Assertions, malformed evidence and missing required executions retain their existing meanings; an externally interrupted execution is incomplete, not a timing failure or a passing test. A separate deterministic resource-limit test may assert a refusal outcome, but elapsed time cannot supply its oracle. The gate's semantic verdict and its list of performance findings are separate outputs (`inv:observe:noninterference`).

**Rule (Budget scope)** · `rule:devperf:scope` Budgets below are initial declared engineering targets on a supported quiet compute worker, not invented performance measurements or promises for every machine. A case budget includes test fixture setup, execution and teardown; a named suite budget includes its shared setup and all planned cases, and a command/gate budget measures inclusive elapsed time without summing overlapping spans. Classify tests by their evidence level and concrete implementation; real-service cases use the real-case budget instead of the pure/mock case budget. Apply both the applicable case and suite budgets, then the command and gate aggregate budgets independently. For each named pure suite, the pure-suite budget applies; mock, PostgreSQL, Elements and combined-service suites use their respective suite rows. Property campaigns include generation and shrinking under declared deterministic case/work limits, never a wall-clock cutoff. Benchmark campaigns have their own suite row. Do not split a workload, change its level or move fixture time outside its span to conceal an excess. Service provisioning, compilation, queue waits and steady execution are separately metered and also remain visible in their enclosing action.

**Rule (Contention and comparison)** · `rule:devperf:comparison` Every budget excess is reported even on a cold or contended run. Diagnose contention from recorded concurrent jobs, CPU quota/throttling or pressure, runnable load, I/O pressure, memory pressure and lock/queue waits; a large wall alone is only suspected contention. Record unavailable indicators as unavailable. Keep cold-cache, queued, contended and quiet measurements distinct; do not raise a quiet budget to fit a noisy worker. A comparable baseline uses 5 scheduled quiet samples with identical workload/configuration and declared cache state; a regression finding records a median increase exceeding both 20 percent and 100 milliseconds. This is a measurement campaign, never an automatic retry of a slow test to obtain a passing result. Preserve every original sample. Incompatible or missing baselines leave the performance comparison inconclusive while the functional verdict remains unchanged; current budget excess still produces its finding. Repeated comparable observations strengthen attribution, not gate authority. Reviewed baseline replacement states the workload/configuration reason and never erases old findings. Scheduling or cache configuration is part of remediation; do not blame code for an unproved contention cause.

**Rule (Build configuration)** · `rule:devperf:builds` A build or aggregate gate budget excess opens a misconfiguration finding as well as its measured warning; classify the responsible phase before changing configuration. Investigate dev/test optimisation, codegen units, debug information, link time, incremental reuse, compiler-cache hit rate, enabled features and dependency weight. Keep clean, warm and one-file-edit measurements separate, and vary one proposed choice at a time with comparable workloads. Preserve debugging utility, all required tests, supported features and the exact release semantics. A gate dominated by a necessary full-corpus check calls for that check's profiling or registry review, not an unrelated compiler flag. Missing measurements are an explicit performance-evidence hold, not a timing-derived failure. Findings remain owned until corrected with evidence or covered by an admitted narrow registry entry; contention classification alone does not close them.

**Decision (Measured profiles)** · `dec:devperf:profiles` The foundation bite measures Cargo's current dev and test defaults, compares candidate settings on clean and incremental builds plus the same test corpus, and records the chosen settings and tradeoffs as explicit decisions with their data. It then changes the profiles and the build-policy checker together under (`amend:toolchain:development-performance`), retaining defaults only where the measurements justify them. No setting is selected merely because it is reputed fast, and no release setting changes here. This resolves the ownership and closing evidence of record 013's open measurement item; its numerical choices remain held for that bite. No profile or build code changes in this record.

**Rule (Expensive-operation admission)** · `rule:devperf:registry` Unavoidably expensive work is admitted only through the dataset-rendered register below. Each entry mints a stable repository label and states its operation and workload, measured wall and provenance, proposed or admitted budget, irreducible work and attempted reductions, placement, owner and review status. Candidate entries track evidence still owed and grant no exception. Admission requires the owner and reviewer to accept the measured residual cost after profiling; merely exceeding a budget, being contended or calling work important is insufficient. An admitted entry changes only that operation's warning threshold and declared placement, never its verdict or evidence obligations; exceeding its own budget still warns and opens a finding. Unregistered excess is a finding, not a back door to timing-based gate failure. Larger service or benchmark runs may live only on suitable workers, but moving them out of the fast path does not waive their required evidence. Withdraw an entry when comparable measurements fit the ordinary budget, updating citations in the same change under (`rule:compatibility:pre-one`). Do not retain an obsolete expensive exception for convenience.
